### Inferences
- A modifier domain such as <name>pouches.com looks available for every name. Registering one early would be cheap.
- Common-word names (TALLY, GAUGE, GRADUATE, QUOTIENT, RATIO) sit in crowded registers, so a food/supplement-class owner is more likely and has to be found on later result pages.
- ALIQUOT and RATIO should be dropped for now because of the class 5 supplement conflicts.

### Gaps
- USPTO TESS/Search, WIPO and Justia were not run. Trademarkia page 1 only. Page 2+ and classes 29/30/32/34 results for TALLY, GAUGE, GRADUATE, JIGGER, CALIPER, DRAM and TARE are unseen.
- Phonetic and variant searches (SKRUPLE, CALIBER, INCREMENTS, TALLEY) were not run.
- Real Google results, and whether any domain is for sale, were not confirmed. Social handles: not checked for any name.

## Meaning and pronunciation (general knowledge, not sourced from the web in this session)

- SCRUPLE: an apothecary unit of 20 grains (about 1.3 g), also a moral qualm. The noun "scruples" reads as ethics and can sound ironic. The risk is a negative reading ("no scruples").
- INCREMENT: a small step up. Abstract, calm.
- CALIPER: a precision measuring tool. Clear, with a slight industrial tone.
- JIGGER: a 1.5 oz bar measure. It suggests cocktails and alcohol, and "jiggers" is also a dialect word for chigger mites.
- TALLY: a count or score. Friendly, easy to say, generic.
- DRAM: a small drink (whisky) and an apothecary unit. Alcohol and Scottish associations; DRAM is also the memory chip term.
- TARE: weight of the container. It sounds like "tear" or "tair", and reads as a verb of subtraction.
- TITRE: French spelling of titer (chemistry); "TEE-ter" or "tighter" are possible readings. Spelling and pronunciation risk, and a crude misreading.
- ALIQUOT: a measured portion. Lab jargon and harder to spell.
- GAUGE: an instrument or "gauging". Also stretched ear piercings in slang.
- GRADUATE: school-related, so it could appeal to students. A minors-appeal risk.
- QUOTIENT, RATIO: math terms, dry.
- TWENTYFIVE: states the dose directly, but is long to type.
- Drug and youth slang was not searched; none of the names is known to me as nicotine-pouch branding, but this is unverified.

## Ranked top 5 (deep screen limited by tool budget; see gaps above)

1. **CALIPER** ("Caliper Pouches"). Precision tool metaphor, adult, easy to spell and say. Risks: live class 35 and class 9/42 marks by Caliper Corporation and Talogy; the earlier class 5 vitamin mark was abandoned; caliper.com and caliperco.com are taken; 84 TM results with pages 2 to 9 unseen. Medical "calipers" association (body fat) is a minor risk.
2. **INCREMENT** ("Increment Pouches" / "Increment Coffee Co."). Dose-step meaning, calm and transparent, with the cleanest first page (13 results, no target-class hits seen). Risks: getincrement.com and increment.com are taken; incrementco.com looked free per RDAP; the abstract word is weak on product imagery; 3 results unseen.
3. **SCRUPLE**. A real apothecary dose unit, distinctive, with no target-class hits on page 1 of 39 results. Risks: the "moral qualm" meaning ("no scruples") can read as a joke or negative; scruple.com and getscruple.com are taken; scruplepouches.com and scrupleco.com looked free per RDAP; SCRUPLES is an established salon brand (class 3) that could draw a likelihood-of-confusion argument in cosmetics only.
4. **JIGGER**. Measure-tool word, memorable. Risks: bar/alcohol cue conflicts with the calm, not-alcohol-adjacent brief; live DOUBLE JIGGER (class 33) and EASY/TIPSY JIGGER (class 9 measuring devices); target-class results on later pages unseen; "jiggers" is also a pest slang word.
5. **TALLY**. Simple and short, "keeping count" fits dose transparency. Risks: 242 TM results with 5/29/30/32 hits unseen; very common word; tally.com owned (not checked for sale); weak distinctiveness.

Dropped: ALIQUOT (pending class 5 supplement application), RATIO (live class 5/29 energy-related supplement), DRAM (alcohol cue plus live class 32 mark), GRADUATE (youth/student skew, crowded register), TITRE (pronunciation), TARE, GAUGE, QUOTIENT, TWENTYFIVE (weaker fit or crowded or long).
