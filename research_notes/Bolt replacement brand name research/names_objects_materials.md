### Meaning / pronunciation notes (my own general knowledge, NOT sourced from this session; verify)
- Rivet: metal fastener; verb "to fascinate". Easy to say/spell. Risk: "riveting" is a cliché; Rivet is a common product word.
- Grommet: eyelet ring; also (from my general knowledge) Australian/UK slang for a young surfer/skater, and "Wallace & Gromit"-adjacent. Youth/cartoon cue, so a poor fit for the no-minors rule. Not verified.
- Hasp: hinged metal fastener for a padlock. Two-syllable-free, one-syllable, easy. Connotes closing/securing a tin lid, fits the tin direction. Drug/youth slang: none known to me, NOT verified. May sound like "gasp"/"hash"-adjacent; check.
- Cotter: cotter pin; also a surname; sounds soft. "Cotter" could read as a person's name.
- Dowel: wooden pin; "dowel" is plain, slightly dull; pronounced "DOW-el".
- Ferrule: metal ring/cap on tools, canes, pencils. Pronounced FAIR-ul; spelling often confused with "ferule"/"ferrel". Hard to spell by ear.
- Pewter: tin alloy; fits tin/metallic look. Common colour name (crowded in paint/finishes).
- Harrar: Ethiopian coffee region (also spelled Harar/Harer). Government of Ethiopia holds a live Class 30 coffee registration; appears blocked for coffee goods.
- Huila: Colombian coffee department; pronounced WEE-la, hard to spell for English speakers.

## Deep screen of the best 5

Selection criteria: no live Class 5/29/30/32/34 hit seen on the Trademarkia page(s) viewed, quiet adult tone, fits the muted metallic / tin direction, one-word and pronounceable. Selection is provisional because most results pages were not viewed.
