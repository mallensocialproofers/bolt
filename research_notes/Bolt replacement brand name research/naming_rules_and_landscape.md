## 1. Name inventory: caffeine, energy, nootropic and nicotine oral-pouch names

### Takeaway
Roughly 40 pouch names were identified. Nicotine brand names cluster in short, hard-consonant or "cool" words (Zyn, Velo, Rogue, Killa, Alp, Fre, Sesh, Zone, Loop), and caffeine brands are mostly short coined or plain words (Wip, Mojo, Grinds, Roon, NZE, Ultra). Any candidate should be screened against both lists. Coverage is incomplete (see Gaps).

## 2. Trademark practice (classes 5, 30, 32, 34; 2(d), 2(e)(1))

### Takeaway
No retrievable USPTO refusal decision for a pouch mark was found, and the TMEP page itself could not be read. What is sourced: caffeine pouch filings appear in Class 5 and Class 34, sometimes alongside nicotine goods in one filing, and descriptiveness is judged against the specific goods.

### Cited Findings
- Descriptiveness is judged in relation to the specific goods, by what the mark immediately conveys about an ingredient, quality, characteristic, function, feature, purpose or use; doubts are resolved for the applicant — per a USPTO form citing TMEP 1209.01(b) and a TTAB summary ([USPTO PTO Form 1957](https://tmng-al.uspto.gov/resting2/api/casedoc/ts/cd/85120345/ROA20110127174717/1/webcontent); UNVERIFIED, secondary excerpt).
- Section 2(f) acquired distinctiveness is the route for a mark refused as merely descriptive — [TTAB opinion, serial 86358219](https://ttabvue.uspto.gov/ttabvue/ttabvue-86358219-EXA-19.pdf) (summary only).
- Grinds and NZE use Class 5 wording "oral pouches with caffeine…" / "caffeine preparations for stimulative use in the form of oral pouches" — [UK IPO 2026/019](https://www.ipo.gov.uk/t-tmj/tm-journals/2026-019/WO0000001898137.html); [UK IPO 2026/016](https://www.ipo.gov.uk/t-tmj/tm-journals/2026-016/WO0000001910480.html).
- UPPERDECKYS lists tobacco-free caffeine pouches with tobacco and nicotine substitutes in Class 34 (filed July 2024); FILTHY POUCHES covers caffeine preparations plus oral nicotine pouches — [Justia](https://trademark.justia.com/986/38/upperdeckys-98638933.html), [Justia](https://trademarks.justia.com/978/69/filthy-97869050.html) (summary only).
- Recent US filings use descriptive wording (PROPER POUCH, ENERGY BY THE POUCH) in Class 5 — [Justia](https://trademarks.justia.com/998/32/proper-99832934.html), [Justia](https://trademarks.justia.com/990/68/energy-by-the-99068971.html). Whether these were refused or disclaimed was not retrievable.

### Inferences
- Because filings mix Class 5 and Class 34 and caffeine with nicotine pouches, a screen should cover all of 5, 30, 32 and 34 and treat nicotine pouch names as potentially "related" (a question for counsel).
- Words like energy, boost, focus, pouch, dose describe the goods and are risky as the distinctive part of a mark. This is a general trademark principle consistent with the descriptiveness test above, not an observed refusal.

### Gaps
- TMEP 1209.01(a)-(c), 1207.01(d) and related-goods guidance not retrieved (uspto.gov URL 404; tmep.uspto.gov returned only the May 2026 foreword). No law-firm explainer retrieved. No actual 2(d) or 2(e)(1) office actions for caffeine words (buzz, alert, boost, dose) found.
- Related-goods analysis for caffeine pouches vs nicotine pouches vs beverages: no authority found.

## 3. Platform risk (Meta, Google, TikTok, payment processors)

### Takeaway
Meta prohibits nicotine pouches and products that simulate smoking even without nicotine; its page does not mention caffeine. Google's policy covers tobacco and smoking-simulating products; whether pouches are covered was not confirmed. Naming-slang risk is not addressed in the retrieved text.

### Cited Findings
- Meta prohibits promoting "the sale or use of tobacco or nicotine products and related paraphernalia", lists nicotine pouches, bars "products that simulate smoking, such as vapes, including products that don't contain tobacco or nicotine", bars promoting tobacco brands, and bars depicting consumption where tobacco use is the ad's primary focus; no mention of caffeine — [Meta Tobacco and Related Products](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/tobacco-related-products/) (retrieved via summarising fetch 2026-10-27).
- Google: summary says the tobacco policy bars ads for tobacco-containing products, components, services facilitating use, and products designed to simulate smoking (herbal and e-cigarettes as examples); no pouch text found; warning at least 7 days before suspension — [Google Ads policy answer 16489929](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16489929) (search summary only, UNVERIFIED). Direct fetch of answer 176031 returned the Healthcare policy instead; 9475042 was 404.
- Fortune notes some schools treat caffeine pouches under "tobacco and simulated tobacco" rules (Holton High School, Kansas) — [Fortune 2026-10-22](https://fortune.com/2026/10/22/schools-ban-caffeine-pouches-students-coffee/).

### Inferences
- The "simulate smoking" language plus school treatment suggests a name or imagery that evokes nicotine pouches may raise review risk. Not confirmed by any platform text.

### Gaps
- TikTok ad policy and Stripe/Shopify/PayPal/high-risk processor wording not retrieved. No source on whether slang terms (snus, dip, lip, hit, puff, vape, kick) trigger review. All of this is UNVERIFIED.

## 4. Youth-appeal red flags and how regulators/schools describe pouches

### Takeaway
Coverage in October 2026 ties caffeine pouches to nicotine pouches in look and to youth slang; there is no federal minimum age.

### Cited Findings
- Nicknames: "lip pillows, lip cushies, and upper or lower deckies"; flavors include coffee, mint, fruit, pumpkin spice; no spit required; resemble nicotine pouches, a key school concern — [Fortune 2026-10-22](https://fortune.com/2026/10/22/schools-ban-caffeine-pouches-students-coffee/).
- No federal minimum purchase age; school nutrition rules do not restrict items brought from home — same Fortune article.
- AAP: some pouches up to 200 mg caffeine, about two cups of coffee; avoiding caffeine is best for children. FDA: about 400 mg/day not generally associated with health problems — same Fortune article.
- Schools banning: Holton High (KS), Peru Central (NY), Onsted Middle (MI), North Middle (WI) — same article.
- Health officials worry pouches can be mistaken for candy or gum (secondary news summaries, UNVERIFIED) — [CP24 2026-10-22](https://www.cp24.com/news/world/2026/10/22/some-us-schools-are-banning-caffeine-pouches-heres-what-parents-and-students-should-know/).
- DCCCA "Energy Pouches vs. Nicotine Pouches Toolkit" (April 2026) exists — [PDF](https://www.dccca.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Energy-Pouches-vs-Nicotine-Pouches-Toolkit.pdf) — contents unreadable.

### Inferences
- Candy, fruit-dessert, cartoon and slang names (including "deck", "lip", "cushie") fit the youth-appeal narrative in current coverage; adult-coded, restrained names fit the adults-only positioning.

### Gaps
- No FDA/FTC statement on caffeine pouch youth marketing found; no state law text.

## 5. FTC/FDA implied claims in names

### Takeaway
FTC judges the net impression of all elements; FDA treats a disease-implying brand name like any claim. Neither source retrieved addresses specific words (energy, focus, calm, sleep, relief); apply by inference.

### Cited Findings
- FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance (Dec 2022) covers express and implied claims for health-related products including foods; net impression of all label/ad elements must be substantiated; the guidance is not binding law — law-firm summaries: [Jones Day](https://jonesday.com/en/insights/2023/01/ftc-overhauls-and-expands-25yearold-health-products-advertising-compliance-guide), [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2023/01/ftcs-expansive-new-advertising-compliance-guidance-covers-marketing-of-any-health-related-product) (UNVERIFIED: ftc.gov text not fetched).
- Brand names conveying disease are treated as claims; 21 CFR 101.93(g)(2) lists implied-disease criteria; structure/function claims require notice to FDA within 30 days, including brand name — [Nutraceuticals World 2013](https://www.nutraceuticalsworld.com/issues/2013-11/view_columns/structurefunction-claims-a-bright-line-rule-or-ambiguity/), [Cornell LII 21 CFR 101.93](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/101.93) (secondary; eCFR not checked).
- One source says pouches are often treated as dietary supplements and not FDA-regulated like OTC drugs; energy pouches regulated as foods or supplements — [Fortune/CP24 summaries](https://www.cp24.com/news/world/2026/10/22/some-us-schools-are-banning-caffeine-pouches-heres-what-parents-and-students-should-know/), [Tobacco Insider (via summary)](https://tobaccoinsider.com/caffeine-pouches/) (UNVERIFIED).

### Inferences
- Names suggesting treatment of a condition (sleep disorder, anxiety, relief, "calm") carry more claim risk than names suggesting a feeling or mood; "sleep" in a stimulant product's name could also create a contradictory net impression. Unsourced reasoning; counsel needed.

### Gaps
- No FDA/FTC page reviewed on words energy/focus/boost/natural/clean/pure; no FDA stance on caffeine pouches as supplements or foods (food-additive status) found.
