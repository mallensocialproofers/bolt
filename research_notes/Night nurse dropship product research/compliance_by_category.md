## 1. Category-by-category regulatory burden (the main comparison)

### Takeaway
Of the nine categories, the verified evidence supports this ordering from lightest to heaviest: apparel/soft goods and digital products (lowest, mostly FTC/advertising and consumer-protection law); non-medical comfort goods and cosmetics under the MoCRA small-business exemption (low to medium, driven by claims discipline and the exemption's $1M test); personal care consumables with any drug or medical-device character, conventional foods, supplements and caffeine (medium to high). The line that matters across categories is the claim, not the product: a compression sock, eye mask or cream becomes a device or drug through what it says it does. The self-care box inherits the burden of its heaviest item.

### Cited Findings
- 21 CFR 880.5780: a "medical support stocking" is "constructed of elastic material and designed to apply controlled pressure to the leg"; if "intended for use in the prevention of pooling of blood in the leg" it is Class II (special controls, exempt from 510(k) subject to 880.9 limits); if intended "for medical purposes other than" that it is Class I. So the device status turns on intended medical purpose plus graduated controlled pressure. — [eCFR 880.5780 (WebFetch summary, HIGH-MEDIUM)](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-21?part=880&section=880.5780), checked 2026-10-27
- FDA clearances for compression/flight socks cite 880.5780 with indications such as helping prevent edema, leg discomfort and DVT for long-distance travelers; i.e. those leg-health claims put a sock in the device pathway. — [FDA 510(k) K032325 PDF](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf3/K032325.pdf) and mirror [Innolitics K062873](https://fda.innolitics.com/device/K062873), 2026-10-27 (secondary mirrors, MEDIUM)
- FDA reissued the General Wellness policy for low-risk devices on January 6, 2026, superseding the Sept 27, 2019 version; notifications should not name a disease or condition or characterize output as abnormal/diagnostic. Search result did not address compression products specifically. — [FDA town hall page](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/medical-devices-news-and-events/town-hall-refresh-general-wellness-policy-low-risk-devices-02112026) and [guidance PDF](https://fda.gov/media/90652/download?attachment=), 2026-10-27 (search summary only, MEDIUM; guidance text not read)
- MoCRA, per FDA: manufacturers/processors must register facilities (renew every two years); the responsible person must list each marketed cosmetic with ingredients (annual update); must substantiate and keep records of safety (no specific tests mandated); must report serious adverse events within 15 business days with a copy of the label; certain small businesses are exempt from GMP, registration and listing; FDA GMP rules not shown as final on that page (draft guidance linked). — [FDA MoCRA page](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-laws-regulations/modernization-cosmetics-regulation-act-2022-mocra), 2026-10-27 (HIGH for what the page says)
- Small-business test (21 USC 364h via Cornell): average yearly US cosmetic sales under $1,000,000 (inflation adjusted) over the previous 3 years; exemption does NOT cover cosmetics that regularly contact the eye mucous membrane, are injected, are for internal use, or alter appearance for more than 24 hours. The exemption is from the registration (364b) and listing (364c) sections; FDA's page also names GMP. — [Cornell LII 21 USC 364h](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/364h), 2026-10-27 (MEDIUM)
- "Responsible person" is the manufacturer, packer or distributor whose name appears on the cosmetic label. — [Cornell LII 21 USC 364](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/364), 2026-10-27 (MEDIUM). Relevance: a brand that puts its name on a dropshipped cream is the responsible person for adverse-event reporting and safety substantiation even if exempt from registration.
- A secondary source says FDA does not issue small-business exemption certificates, so the burden of proof is on the business. — [Registrar Corp MoCRA exemptions](https://www.registrarcorp.com/blog/cosmetics/mocra/mocra-exemptions/), 2026-10-27 (UNVERIFIED; sources in the same search disagreed on dates and on whether listing is exempt)
- Dietary supplements: structure/function statements must not be disease claims; "disease" means damage to an organ, part, structure or system so it does not function properly, and (g)(2)(iii) reaches an "abnormal condition associated with a natural state or process" if uncommon or potentially significantly harmful; notification to FDA within 30 days of first marketing a statement; disclaimer "This statement has not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease." The text read does not mention sleep or insomnia. — [eCFR 21 CFR 101.93 (WebFetch summary)](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-21?part=101&section=101.93), 2026-10-27 (MEDIUM)
- Retail-style columnist account: a "sleep aid" label was flagged by Amazon as implying insomnia treatment; supplements may make structure/function claims with disclaimer. — [The Beacon](https://www.thebeaconnewspapers.com/what-supplements-can-and-cant-claim), 2026-10-27 (UNVERIFIED, commentary)
- FDA food facility registration: facilities that manufacture, process, pack or hold food for US consumption must register and renew every other year; the page read did not state exemptions or supplement treatment. — [FDA registration page](https://www.fda.gov/food/guidance-regulation-food-and-dietary-supplements/registration-food-facilities-and-other-submissions), 2026-10-27 (HIGH for what it says; exemptions not confirmed)
- Hand sanitizer: FDA treats consumer antiseptic rubs under its topical-antiseptic OTC drug framework; a compliance-vendor page says only ethanol, isopropyl alcohol and benzalkonium chloride are allowed in the monograph path. — [FDA topical antiseptic page](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/over-counter-otc-nonprescription-drugs/topical-antiseptic-drug-products) (listed in results, not opened) and [reach24h](https://en.reach24h.com/service/disinfectant-biocide/us-fda-registration-hand-sanitizer), 2026-10-27 (UNVERIFIED)
- Meta Health and Wellness policy: dietary supplements/weight-loss products must be targeted at 18+; supplements ads allowed for adults, including showing use and effects; exempt from 18+ requirement: general wellbeing, general foods, non-permanent cosmetics such as makeup or creams; no negative-self-perception statements about appearance, body parts or hygiene; no cure claims for listed incurable diseases; clickbait/exaggerated outcome claims barred in health contexts. — [Meta Transparency Center, Health and Wellness](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness/), 2026-10-27 (HIGH-MEDIUM)
- Meta Drugs and Pharmaceuticals and Privacy/Personal-Attributes policy pages exist (not opened). — [Meta Drugs & Pharma](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/drugs-pharmaceuticals), 2026-10-27 (UNVERIFIED)

## 2. Nurse-targeted marketing across categories

### Takeaway
The FTC Endorsement Guides make both the brand and nurse creators responsible for disclosure and for claims, and an endorser presented as a nurse "expert" must really have and exercise that expertise. Meta's policy prohibits ads that assert or imply the viewer's health condition, which a "tired night nurse" angle can drift into.

### Cited Findings
- Material connections (payments, free product, affiliate links) must be disclosed when a significant minority of the audience would not expect them; disclosure must be clear and conspicuous, and in social media "unavoidable". — [16 CFR 255.5 / Part 255 (WebFetch summary)](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-16?part=255), 2026-10-27 (MEDIUM)
- Expert endorsement: the endorser must have the expertise claimed and the endorsement must rest on an actual exercise of that expertise (255.3). — same source
- Advertisers can be liable for deceptive endorsements even if the endorser is not, should guide and monitor endorsers; endorsers can be liable for undisclosed connections and for claims beyond personal experience; ads must substantiate claims conveyed through endorsements; consumer testimonials are not scientific evidence; fake or paid positive reviews are deceptive. Federal Register citation given: 88 FR 48102 (July 26, 2023). — same source
- Meta ad standards bar content that "asserts or implies" personal attributes including physical or mental health (medical conditions); the example given is that second-person references like "Do you have diabetes?" are prohibited. — [Meta Privacy Violations and Personal Attributes](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/privacy-violations-personal-attributes/) via search summary, 2026-10-27 (MEDIUM; page itself not opened)
- Meta health policy bars statements attacking appearance or hygiene and clickbait outcome claims. — [Meta Health and Wellness](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness/), 2026-10-27 (HIGH-MEDIUM)

### Inferences
- Copy such as "Tired after your night shift?" or "Your swollen feet..." could read as asserting a condition about the viewer; safer copy talks about the product ("scrub caps with a soft band") not about the viewer's state. Needs a Meta policy read by counsel or the platform's own review.
- A creator who is a nurse can be an "expert" only for things her nursing training actually covers; a product-effectiveness opinion on a skin cream or sock is probably a consumer endorsement, and any health claim she makes becomes the brand's substantiation problem.
- Ads must not use nurse credentials or "RN" to imply medical endorsement of a product; it is the combination of House Rule 4 (no health claims) and the Expert Endorsement rule that makes "nurse-approved" a high-risk phrase.

### Gaps
- State nursing-board rules on use of the RN title in commercial advertising: no primary source found this pass; UNVERIFIED that any state restricts it.
- ANA, AACN, ANCC and similar organizations' marks and name-use policies: not searched; do not use their names, logos or "accredited/endorsed by" language without written permission; treat "nurse"/"RN" in a product name as an open trademark-clearance question (USPTO search was not run).
- TikTok and Google Ads health-adjacent product policies: not researched this pass (only the Delphine rulebook's nicotine-pouch findings exist).
- Meta policy on targeting by profession: not confirmed. The personal-attributes page fetch failed.

## 3. Subscriptions: ROSCA, state auto-renewal laws, click-to-cancel status

### Takeaway
As of the sources found, the FTC's 2024 "click-to-cancel" amendments were vacated (8th Cir., July 8, 2025) and the Negative Option Rule restored to its pre-2024 form (Feb 12, 2026); an ANPRM followed (comments closed April 13, 2026) with no later proposed rule found. ROSCA and state laws, especially California, still apply.

### Cited Findings
- ROSCA (15 USC 8403) for Internet negative-option charges: disclose all material terms clearly and conspicuously before collecting billing info; obtain express informed consent before charging; provide simple mechanisms to stop recurring charges. — [Cornell LII 15 USC 8403](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/8403), 2026-10-27 (MEDIUM)
- Eighth Circuit vacated the amended Negative Option Rule on July 8, 2025 (Custom Communications v. FTC) for lack of a preliminary regulatory analysis. — [Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/eighth-circuit-vacates-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule) (UNVERIFIED secondary); consistent with the Federal Register notice below
- FTC final rule published Feb 12, 2026 restores the Negative Option Rule to its pre-2024 form; ANPRM published March 13, 2026 (91 FR 12318), comments due April 13, 2026; the current rule reaches only prenotification plans, with continuity plans, automatic renewals and free-to-pay conversions reached through Section 5, ROSCA and the Telemarketing Sales Rule. — [Federal Register, Feb 12, 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/12/2026-02866/revision-of-the-negative-option-rule-withdrawal-of-the-cars-rule-removal-of-the-non-compete-rule-to) and [Federal Register, Mar 13, 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/03/13/2026-04952/rule-concerning-the-use-of-prenotification-negative-option-plans), 2026-10-27 (MEDIUM; search-result summary, pages not opened). FTC info-collection notice dated Oct 14, 2026 not read.
- California AB 2863 amended Bus. & Prof. Code 17601/17602 effective July 1, 2025: express affirmative consent to renewal terms; free-to-paid conversions covered; annual reminders; cancellation in the same medium used to sign up; broader misrepresentation reach. — [Barnes & Thornburg](https://btlaw.com/en/insights/alerts/2025/california-expands-automatic-renewal-law-new-requirements-now-in-effect), [Fenwick](https://fenwick.com/insights/publications/california-tightens-requirements-for-automatically-renewing-subscriptions), 2026-10-27 (UNVERIFIED law-firm alerts; statute text not retrieved, 403)

### Inferences
- A subscription box should be built to the strictest state (California) and ROSCA together: separate unchecked consent, price/term/frequency disclosed before payment, email confirmation, one-click online cancel, annual reminder, easy pause/skip.
- Subscription of consumables (supplements, creams) adds health-claim risk to every recurring send.

### Gaps
- Other states' auto-renewal laws (NY, IL, VT, MN, VA, etc.): not researched; need a 50-state survey by counsel.
- Whether any FTC proposed rule followed the ANPRM after April 2026: none found.
- Payment-processor and Shopify subscription app terms: not read.

## Questions for counsel and other professionals (For a professional)
1. FDA: for any compression product, what claims, pressure levels and labeling keep a sock outside 21 CFR 880.5780? Is the 2026 General Wellness policy relevant at all to a passive textile?
2. MoCRA: does the brand qualify as exempt under the $1M three-year test, does a dropshipped product from a supplier make the brand the responsible person, and what adverse-event contact and fragrance-allergen labeling is required now?

7. Subscriptions: 50-state auto-renewal review and a compliant cancellation flow; any residual ROSCA or processor risks.
