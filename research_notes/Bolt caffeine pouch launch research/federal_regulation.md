# US Federal Regulation of Oral Caffeine Pouches (Bolt, 25 mg/pouch, online DTC + wholesale)

Date checked for all items: 2026-10-27 unless noted. Findings and questions for counsel only; nothing here states any practice is legal, compliant or safe. Research used ~16 tool calls; many topics are only partly verified (see Gaps).

## 1. How FDA treats caffeine pouches today; enforcement

### Takeaway
I found no FDA statement, warning letter or rule aimed specifically at caffeine pouches. Pouches are being described (by trade/vendor/press sources, not FDA) as dietary supplements/food, outside FDA tobacco authority. FDA's only on-point caffeine-supplement enforcement I could verify is the 2015-2021 pure/liquid/powder caffeine letters and the 2018 guidance.

### Cited Findings
- No warning letter about caffeine pouches surfaced in search of FDA warning letters; results were powdered/liquid caffeine letters (2015 distributors; Liquid Caffeine 2018; Trippo International 2021 for 16 oz "Super Caffeine (Anhydrous)"). Search summary only, pouch absence is UNVERIFIED against the full FDA database — [Trippo 2021](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/trippo-international-llc-612586-03102021), [Liquid Caffeine 2018](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/liquidcaffeinecom-545456-05292018) (checked 10-27-2026, via search snippets, letter text not read)
- FDA's 2018 letters and guidance rest on adulteration under FD&C Act 402(f)(1)(A) ("significant or unreasonable risk of illness or injury" under recommended/ordinary conditions of use) — [FDA guidance page](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/guidance-industry-highly-concentrated-caffeine-dietary-supplements)
- Vendor/trade sources state caffeine pouches are not tobacco products, are not subject to PMTA, and fall under supplement/food rules with no FDA premarket approval — [Tobacco Insider](https://tobaccoinsider.com/caffeine-pouches-questions-and-answers/) and a retailer blog (nectr.energy) — UNVERIFIED (secondary; Tobacco Insider page returned empty on fetch, only search snippet seen)
- July 2026 Pharmacist's Letter says pouches are often sold as supplements, not regulated like OTC drugs, so labeled caffeine may not be accurate; says pouches contain 50-225 mg each; names no enforcement — [Pharmacist's Letter](https://pharmacist.therapeuticresearch.com/Content/nm/Newsletters/July2026/Caffeine-Pouches-The-New-Nicotine)
- AP report Oct 22, 2026: schools banning pouches; some contain up to 200 mg (per AAP); no federal minimum purchase age; named maker ALP Pouch declined comment — [CP24/AP](https://www.cp24.com/news/world/2026/10/22/some-us-schools-are-banning-caffeine-pouches-heres-what-parents-and-students-should-know/)
- A single-source Food Safety Magazine item reports FDA Commissioner Makary was ousted, with Kyle Diamantas reportedly replacing him — UNVERIFIED, leadership context only — via search result, [food-safety.com HFP tag](https://www.food-safety.com/articles/keyword/2554-human-foods-program)

### Inferences
- Bolt at 25 mg is far below the 50-225 mg range reported for other pouches, which may matter to a risk narrative; but several uses/day multiplies exposure (counsel question: how does FDA assess "recommended conditions of use" for a multi-use-per-day product?).
- Rising press/school attention (Oct 2026) increases chance of FDA or congressional attention; no action found yet.

### Gaps
- No search of FDA's warning letter database or FDA constituent updates directly for "pouch" (database not queryable via tools used). Counsel/ops should run it.
- Whether FDA treats caffeine pouches as a conventional food (caffeine as unapproved food additive/GRAS question) vs dietary supplement was not found from any FDA source. Pouches placed in the mouth, not swallowed, raise a "dietary supplement" definition question (21 USC 321(ff), ingested) — not researched; question for counsel.

## 2. FDA 2018 guidance, 400 mg/day, caffeine-labeling guidance

### Takeaway
2018 guidance is final, non-binding, aimed at pure/highly concentrated caffeine supplements (bulk powder/liquid). FDA cites 400 mg/day as not generally associated with negative effects in healthy adults. A draft "Labeling Caffeine Content in Foods and Beverages" guidance was added to FDA's 2026 agenda on June 29, 2026; no text or dates yet.

### Cited Findings
- Guidance "Highly Concentrated Caffeine in Dietary Supplements," issued April 2018, status Final, docket FDA-2018-D-1189; covers supplements made of or containing only pure/highly concentrated caffeine; non-binding; page contains no 400 mg figure or serving limit — [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/guidance-industry-highly-concentrated-caffeine-dietary-supplements)
- Press coverage of the guidance: concern was bulk packaging (>10,000 servings), home-measuring risk; ~1 tsp powder ≈ 3,200 mg, ½ cup liquid ≈ 2,000 mg — [Nutritional Outlook (secondary)](https://nutritionaloutlook.com/fda-issues-guidance-over-highly-concentrated-caffeine-dietary-supplements); guidance said to be effective immediately — UNVERIFIED beyond search summary
- FDA consumer update: FDA "has cited 400 milligrams a day" as "an amount not generally associated with negative effects"; sensitivity varies; pregnant/breastfeeding people should talk to a provider; avoid caffeinated drinks for children under 2 — [FDA consumer update](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much)
- FDA 2026 Human Foods Program guidance agenda (posted June 29, 2026) lists new topic "Labeling Caffeine Content in Foods and Beverages; Draft Guidance for Industry"; page gives no details or dates — [FDA constituent update](https://www.fda.gov/food/hfp-constituent-updates/fda-releases-2026-human-foods-program-guidance-agenda)
- Trade report: HFP "will highlight industry best practices for labeling added caffeine content"; agenda is a priority list not a commitment; no proposed language, covered products or deadlines; today added caffeine must appear in the ingredient list but mg need not be stated for conventional foods; article does not mention pouches — [Daily Coffee News, Jul 17 2026](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2026/07/17/fda-eyes-caffeine-labeling-as-a-2026-priority/)

### Inferences
- A 25 mg pouch is not "pure or highly concentrated caffeine" in bulk, but the Section 402(f)(1)(A) risk theory still applies to any supplement; the 2018 guidance is a signal of FDA reasoning, not a safe harbor.
- Whether the new labeling guidance covers supplements (vs "foods and beverages") is unknown.

### Gaps
- Whether the draft guidance has been published since June 29, 2026 (not found; check Federal Register/regulations.gov).
- Whether the guidance document itself addresses single-serve products (full text not read).

## 3. Label requirements

### Takeaway
If marketed as a dietary supplement, label needs a Supplement Facts panel, with cGMP under 21 CFR 111. I verified only 21 CFR 101.36 in this pass (via eCFR); other items below are from general knowledge and marked for verification.

### Cited Findings
- 21 CFR 101.36: panel headed "Supplement Facts"; serving size under 101.9(b)/101.12(b); servings per container (may be omitted if net quantity states it); ingredients without RDI/DRV (caffeine would fall here) listed by common name in metric units with a "Daily Value not established" footnote symbol; title larger than other text; hairline box; body text at least 8 pt (headings/footnotes may be 6 pt); proprietary-blend rule; small-package exemptions in paragraph (h) — [eCFR 21 CFR 101.36](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-10-20/title-21.xml?part=101&section=101.36) (checked via eCFR API snapshot dated 2026-10-20)
- Conventional food contrast: added caffeine must appear in ingredient list; mg generally not required — [Daily Coffee News](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2026/07/17/fda-eyes-caffeine-labeling-as-a-2026-priority/) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Under a supplement path, caffeine amount per serving would appear in the panel; "serving" definition (one pouch vs multiple) is a design/legal decision tied to the multi-use-per-day claim.

### Gaps (not verified; counsel/primary check needed)
- Warning statements: no federal mandatory caffeine warning for supplements found; FDCA 403/21 CFR 101.5 (name and place of business), 101.9, statement of identity, net quantity, "structure/function" disclaimer 21 CFR 101.93 — not fetched.
- Allergen labeling (FALCPA/FASTER Act; sesame, 9 allergens) — not fetched.
- cGMP 21 CFR Part 111, adverse event reporting (serious AE, FD&C 761) and domestic contact info on label — not fetched.
- NDI notification (21 USC 350b) — caffeine marketed pre-1994 so likely not "new," but the pouch delivery form is untested; question for counsel; not researched.
- GRAS status of caffeine: 21 CFR 182.1180 limits caffeine in cola-type beverages; relevance to pouches (if treated as food) not researched.
- FDA label guidance for pouch product formats: none found.

## 4. FTC: advertising, substantiation, endorsements, reviews, subscriptions

### Takeaway
Standard FTC rules apply. The Reviews Rule is in force since Oct 21, 2024. Click-to-Cancel was vacated; FTC began a new rulemaking in 2026; ROSCA still applies.

### Cited Findings
- FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance: health claims need "competent and reliable scientific evidence" (generally randomized controlled human trials); advertisers liable for misleading endorsements including influencers; material connections must be disclosed clearly; "results not typical" does not cure; structure/function claims need no FTC notification but need substantiation; DSHEA disclaimer won't cure deception. Replaces 1998 supplement guide. Last 28k chars of page not read — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance)
- Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR 465): effective Oct 21, 2024; bans fake reviews/testimonials, buying sentiment-conditioned reviews, undisclosed insider reviews, company-controlled "independent" review sites, review suppression, fake social influence indicators; courts may impose civil penalties for knowing violations (dollar amount not stated on page) — [FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)
- FTC Negative Option page: Feb 12, 2026 Federal Register notice proposed revising Negative Option Rule (and withdrawing CARS rule); Mar 11, 2026 request for comment; Mar 13, 2026 ANPRM, docket FTC-2026-0265; 2024 final rule published Nov 15, 2024; page does not state court outcome or ROSCA — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/negative-option-rule)
- Law-firm lead: Eighth Circuit vacated the 2024 click-to-cancel amendments in July 2025; FTC continues to enforce ROSCA case-by-case — [BCLP](https://www.bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/im-not-dead-yet-is-the-demise-of-the-ftcs-click-to-cancel-exaggerated.html) — UNVERIFIED against the court opinion
- 16 CFR 255 (Endorsement Guides, 2023 revision): not fetched in this pass; the Health Products guidance above summarizes endorsement principles.

### Inferences
- If Bolt offers subscribe-and-save on Shopify, ROSCA (clear terms, express consent, simple cancellation) applies regardless of the Click-to-Cancel status.
- "Several uses a day" marketing + any energy/focus/performance claims need substantiation; claims like "safe alternative to nicotine" would also invite risk.

### Gaps
- ROSCA statutory text (15 USC 8401-8405) not fetched; Eighth Circuit opinion not read; State auto-renewal laws out of scope.
- Any pending Click-to-Cancel final action date after the March 2026 ANPRM: not found.

## 5. Federal age limits, bills, tobacco authority, congressional/FDA letters

### Takeaway
No federal minimum age for caffeine pouches found. No bill specifically on caffeine pouches found. The Sarah Katz Caffeine Safety Act (H.R. 2511) is the main federal caffeine bill.

### Cited Findings
- AP (Oct 22, 2026): "no federal minimum purchase age" for caffeine pouches; school districts (NY, KS, PA, MO) adding pouches to conduct policies; Holton HS (KS) treats under tobacco/simulated-tobacco rules — [CP24/AP](https://www.cp24.com/news/world/2026/10/22/some-us-schools-are-banning-caffeine-pouches-heres-what-parents-and-students-should-know/)
- Federal 21+ law (Tobacco 21) reaches tobacco/nicotine products, not caffeine-only pouches (vendor blog; UNVERIFIED) — [Roon](https://www.takeroon.com/blog/how-old-to-buy-caffeine-pouches)
- H.R. 2511, Sarah Katz Caffeine Safety Act, 119th Congress, reintroduced Mar 31, 2025 (sponsor Rep. Robert Menendez Jr., D-NJ per search summary); would require caffeine labeling on packaged foods and supplements, safety reviews, education campaign; no pouch mention — [PolicyRisk](https://policyrisk.com/legislation/HR2511), [Perkins Coie via Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/healthcare/1664148/caffeine-safety-legislation-introduced-in-congress) — secondary, status not confirmed on congress.gov
- Past congressional pressure on caffeine: 2015 six senators urged FDA to ban pure caffeine sales — [New Hope](https://www.newhope.com/regulatory/6-senators-urge-fda-to-ban-pure-caffeine-sales)

### Inferences
- Nicotine-pouch legislation or "pouch" age-restriction proposals could be drafted by product form; I did not find any that sweep in caffeine.

### Gaps
- congress.gov search for 2026 bills mentioning "caffeine pouch" or "oral pouch" not done; no Congress/FDA letter on caffeine pouches found; state laws not researched (out of federal scope).
