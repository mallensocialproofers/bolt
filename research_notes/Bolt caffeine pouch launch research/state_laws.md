# US State and Local Laws Affecting Caffeine Pouches (25 mg, adults-only, online DTC + wholesale) — checked 2026-10-27

SCOPE/LIMITS: ~14 tool calls, mostly secondary sources via search snippets and a few fetches. State-by-state primary-source review was NOT completed. Every row below is UNVERIFIED unless a primary source is linked. Nothing here concludes legality; see counsel questions at the end.

## 1. Which states have enacted or proposed laws specifically on caffeine pouches, energy pouches, or caffeine products sold to minors?

### Takeaway
No enacted US state law specifically on caffeine pouches was found. The only 2026 bill found that could touch them is Delaware HB 394 ("caffeinated dietary supplements"), which passed the House as a signage-only substitute with the age ban removed. Older narrower laws exist for powdered pure caffeine (Illinois; Suffolk County NY).

### Cited Findings
- Delaware HB 394 (original, filed ~April/May 2026, Rep. DeShanna Neal) would have barred retailers from selling energy drinks or caffeinated dietary supplements to anyone under 18; energy drink defined as at least 100 mg caffeine per 8 oz; companion HB 396 would require warning symbols on menus for drinks over 100 mg/serving (per the House GOP release; I could not load the page directly, details came via search snippet) — [DE House GOP](https://housegop.delaware.gov/2026/05/02/caffeine-regulation-bills-delaware/)
- HB 394 House Substitute 1: age ban removed; Division of Public Health to create signage (not recommended for children, caffeine-sensitive, pregnant/nursing); retailers "encouraged" not required to display; passed House unanimously ~June 11-12, 2026; sent to Senate (sponsors Neal and Sen. Lockman); no Senate action found — [DE House Dems, 6/12/26](https://housedems.delaware.gov/2026/06/12/house-passes-neal-bill-to-better-regulate-highly-caffeinated-beverages/); [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/delawares-house-representatives-passes-caffeine-warning-signage-bill). Neither source says whether pouches fall within "caffeinated dietary supplements"; the term is undefined in the sources read.
- Illinois 410 ILCS 647 (Public Act 99-0050, eff. 1/1/2016) bans sale of "powdered pure caffeine" (loose powder) to under-18s; Class A misdemeanor, repeat Class 4 felony — [ILGA](https://ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/documents/041006470K10.htm), [PA 99-0050](https://ilga.gov/ftp/Public%20Acts/99/099-0050.htm). Pouch format likely outside this definition (my inference).
- Suffolk County NY local law bans sale of pure caffeine products to minors — [CBS NY](https://www.cbsnews.com/newyork/news/suffolk-county-votes-to-ban-sales-of-powered-caffeine-to-minors/) (date/current status not verified).
- Energy-drink minor-sales bills reported in MA (H.1908, filed 1/15/2025, under-18, fines $50/$100/$250), CT, NY, IN; current status not verified — [MA H1908](https://malegislature.gov/Bills/194/H1908.Html); secondary summary via search only for CT/NY/IN.
- Weight-loss/muscle-building supplement age laws: New York (S 5823, signed 2023, effective ~April 2024, under-18 ban; Second Circuit reportedly upheld; CRN cert petition reported) — secondary sources only (Vitamin Retailer/Venable via search). California reportedly enacted a similar law (AB 2030 reported signed 9/28/2026 by one search snippet; bill number and date UNVERIFIED, may be wrong). Pending reported: Michigan HB 5250 (introduced 11/12/2025, House Health Policy; under-18; $1,000 fines; online age verification; caffeine not named) — [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/11/new-michigan-bill-targets-youth-access-to-weight-loss-and-muscle-building-supplements); NJ A 5487 (reported introduced 9/14/2026, UNVERIFIED); RI S 2774 (2026, UNVERIFIED). I could not load NY GBL 391-oo text (nysenate.gov returned not found), so whether a 25 mg caffeine pouch is "weight loss/muscle building" depends on marketing claims and is unconfirmed.
- Federal: no minimum purchase age; not under Tobacco 21 because no nicotine — [Vaping360](https://vaping360.com/learn/how-old-to-buy-caffeine-pouches/) (search snippet; page returned 403 on fetch); H.R. 2511 Sarah Katz Caffeine Safety Act introduced, not enacted — [Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/2511). FDA reportedly put caffeine labeling on 2026 agenda (law-firm blog, lead only).

### Inferences
- Supplement-vs-food classification and marketing claims will drive which age laws apply; avoid "weight loss/fat burn/muscle" claims.
- Self-imposed 18+ (or 21+) with online age verification is consistent with every pending bill found.

### Gaps
- Per-state legislature searches were not run; Delaware HB 394 Senate status and exact definitions unconfirmed (legis.delaware.gov fetch gave wrong page); HB 396 status unknown; CT/NY/IN/MA bill numbers/status unverified; CA/NJ/RI numbers unverified.

## 2. Tobacco/nicotine "oral pouch" laws that could sweep in caffeine pouches

### Takeaway
No definition clearly capturing or excluding caffeine was confirmed. Pending/enacted nicotine-pouch definitions seen are nicotine-based (e.g., NY S9954 "smokeless pre-portioned pouch containing nicotine but no tobacco"), which on their face would not cover caffeine-only pouches, but "oral product"/"tobacco-like" or flavor-ban language could vary by state and was not reviewed.

### Cited Findings
- NY S9954 (introduced 4/17/2026) defines nicotine pouch as containing nicotine; no caffeine mention in text seen — [NY Senate](https://legislation.nysenate.gov/pdf/bills/2025/S9954); similar A141/S443 — [A141A](https://legislation.nysenate.gov/pdf/bills/2025/A141A). Status unverified.
- Louisiana HB 876 (2024) digest: excludes oral nicotine pouch from "alternative nicotine product" — [LA Legis](https://www.legis.la.gov/Legis/ViewDocument.aspx?d=1361069) (enactment unverified; says nothing on caffeine).
- DC: 2022 flavored tobacco/nicotine ban enforced against Zyn ($1.2M settlement) — [DC OAG](https://oag.dc.gov/release/attorney-general-schwalb-announces-zyn). Nicotine-based; caffeine applicability not found.
- California AB 3218 reportedly added synthetic nicotine to flavored tobacco law (advocacy source, UNVERIFIED) — [Lungs R Us](https://lungsrus.org/nicotine-pouches/).
- Retail-risk note: no source found showing PACT Act or state tobacco-license regimes apply to non-nicotine products.

### Inferences
- Risk is practical rather than textual: look-alike packaging/branding to nicotine pouches and shared shelf/retailer policies; some districts treat pouches as tobacco-simulating (Holton HS, KS).

### Gaps
- No 50-state review of tobacco-product definitions, "tobacco-like"/"simulated tobacco" or look-alike provisions; PACT/shipping rules not researched.

## 3. State AG, school-district, or local actions

### Takeaway
Action found is at school level and county level (powdered caffeine); no AG action against caffeine pouches found. Texas AG opened an energy-drink investigation, not pouches.

### Cited Findings
- Schools: Peru CSD (NY) code of conduct bans caffeine pills/pouches; Holton HS (KS) treats under tobacco/simulated tobacco rules; districts in NY, KS, PA, MO and Onsted MS (MI), North MS (WI) warned/restricted — [Fortune 10/22/2026](https://fortune.com/2026/10/22/schools-ban-caffeine-pouches-students-coffee/)
- Texas AG Paxton investigation of Celsius/Alani Nu energy drinks announced 6/4/2026 — [Troutman AG Monitor 6/18/26](https://www.troutman.com/insights/troutman-pepper-locke-state-attorneys-general-monitor-june-18-2026/) (snippet; not pouches)
- Suffolk County NY pure caffeine local law (above).

### Gaps
- No city/county ordinance on pouches found; no AG letters on pouches found (searches: "caffeine pouches" + attorney general/legislature/city council).

## 4. Tax and supplement registration (high level)

3. Do NY/CA (and other) weight-loss/muscle-building age laws reach the product given our claims (energy/focus vs performance)?

5. Prop 65 and state sales/excise tax treatment; wholesale licensing; online age-verification standard to adopt (18+ vs 21+)?
6. Marketing and packaging look-alike risk to nicotine pouch brands and school/minor-appeal flavors.
