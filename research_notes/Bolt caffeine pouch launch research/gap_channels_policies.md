## What the official policies say (per channel)

### Takeaway
Only 3 of ~22 requested channels yielded verifiable official wording (Google tobacco, Google unapproved substances, USPS Pub 52 sec. 47). None of the verified text names caffeine pouches or "nicotine-free alternatives". Everything else is UNVERIFIED and must be confirmed directly (logged-in account, support ticket, or counsel).

### Cited Findings

**Google Ads: Tobacco and related products (VERIFIED partially, checked 2026-10-27)**
- Prohibits "ads for tobacco or any products containing tobacco"; also component/facilitating products and products "designed to simulate tobacco smoking". Examples listed: "Cigarettes, cigars, snus, chewing tobacco, rolling tobacco, pipe tobacco"; "Herbal cigarettes, electronic cigarettes, e-cigarettes". "A warning will be issued at least 7 days prior to any suspension of your account." Page did not name oral/nicotine pouches or nicotine-free alternatives in the portion returned (page was truncated). — [Google Ads Tobacco policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16489929?hl=en)
- Anecdote only (not policy): a Haypp Group-related commenter says sponsored nicotine-pouch ads appear in Google search. — [Substack comments](https://invariant.substack.com/p/haypp-group-further/comments)

**Google Ads: Healthcare and medicines / Unapproved substances (PARTIAL; wording via search summary, checked 2026-10-27)**

Violations: "account suspension upon detection and without prior warning" (summary of page, not verbatim). Caffeine not named in what was retrieved. My own fetch of the page returned only "Recreational drugs (chemical or herbal); psychoactive substances; equipment to facilitate drug use" under Dangerous products, and the section list including "Unapproved substances" and "Addiction services". — [Healthcare and medicines](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031)

**USPS Publication 52 (VERIFIED partially, checked 2026-10-27)**
- Sec. 47 definitions: cigarette = "Any roll of tobacco wrapped in paper or in any substance not containing tobacco"; smokeless tobacco = "Any finely cut, ground, powdered, or leaf tobacco intended to be placed in the oral or nasal cavity"; ENDS = "Any electronic device that delivers nicotine, flavor, or any other substance through an aerosolized solution." Min age to send or receive covered products: "21 years old". Nicotine pouches are not named; caffeine pouches are tobacco-free and non-nicotine so these definitions do not obviously apply. Sec. 472-474 (nonmailable, exceptions) text not retrieved. — [Pub 52 sec. 47](https://pe.usps.com/text/pub52/pub52c4_026.htm)

### Inferences
- Verified Google/USPS text is framed around tobacco-containing, nicotine-delivering or simulated-smoking products; a tobacco-free, nicotine-free pouch is not named, but the look-alike format could trigger automated review (Roon-affiliated source says automated review can flag creatives resembling nicotine-pouch ads — brand-affiliated, low reliability).
- Question for counsel/processor: does the product's presentation as a "pouch" bring it within "products designed to simulate" or processors' "tobacco/e-cig/age-restricted" categories, even without nicotine?

### Gaps
- All items marked UNVERIFIED above. Recommend direct logged-in checks or support tickets, and asking each processor for written confirmation for "caffeine oral pouch, 25 mg, 21+ gating".

## Public examples of caffeine-pouch brands rejected or banned

### Takeaway
No credible public report found of a specific brand being rejected or banned by an ad platform, marketplace or processor.

### Cited Findings
- Search for Wip/Nectr/Roon/Mojo/Ultra/Grinds with Meta/Google/TikTok bans found none. — multiple searches 2026-10-27.
- Epoch Times (Chinese) Jan 2026 reports Health Canada bans sales of such products and says Amazon removed some brands while others remain; not verified, non-US. — [Epoch Times](https://www.epochtimes.com/gb/26/1/4/n14669097.htm)
- Roon (brand-published, Helios Intelligence, Inc.) states a nicotine-free product "is eligible, as a matter of published ad policy, to run standard paid ads on Meta, YouTube, and Twitch" "subject to each platform's general ad policies"; conflicts with a third-party claim that Meta bars even nicotine-free alternatives. Self-serving; Meta page already covered by another researcher. — [Roon blog](https://www.takeroon.com/blog/nicotine-pouch-streamer-sponsorship-ad-restrictions); [AdAmigo](https://www.adamigo.ai/blog/meta-ads-policy-tobacco-ad-guidelines-2025)
- Retail/age context: Nectr requires 18+ online; Mojo says no age restriction but not recommended under 18 (NBC). Wip CEO is an ex-Juul marketing executive. 200 mg Wip pouch ~ two cups of coffee per FDA guidance cited by NBC. — [NBC New York](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/national-international/cross-between-nicotine-pouch-energy-drink-caffeine-pouches/6341787/)

### Inferences
- Regulatory attention is on teen marketing, so an adult-only, age-gated posture and a 21+ standard would be more conservative than competitors' 18+.

### Gaps
- Which processors/ad channels Nectr, Roon, Wip, Mojo, Ultra, Grinds actually use: no evidence found (checkout fingerprinting, e.g. inspecting their stores' payment scripts, was not done). Ultra and Grinds returned no relevant coverage.
