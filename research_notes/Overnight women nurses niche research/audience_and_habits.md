# Overnight women nurses: audience portrait and habits (US, checked 2026-10-27)

Method note: Firecrawl unavailable; used WebSearch and WebFetch (about 20 calls). Age policy: treated as 21+ because none is recorded in work/decisions.md. Many sources returned 402/403 (ACOG, Becker's, CDC) and Reddit, allnurses, TikTok and Facebook could not be reached, so there are NO verbatim nurse quotes in this file. Items marked UNVERIFIED are search-summary level, competitor-authored or secondary. All "date checked" = 2026-10-27. Every marketing observation below is about habits and framing only; nothing here is a claim to make.

## Demographics, shifts, pay and schedules

### Takeaway
RNs are overwhelmingly women (about 89%, secondary source), median age 50, and about half work in hospitals. Primary data on the share of RNs on nights, travel or per-diem was NOT found; that share has to stay an open number.

### Cited Findings
- US RN headcount about 3,465,400 (2025) and median pay $97,550 (May 2025); hospital and nursing-care nurses work shifts around the clock including nights, weekends, holidays — [BLS OOH RNs](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/registered-nurses.htm), as recorded in the existing niche_occupational.md note, checked 2026-10-27 (not re-fetched here).
- NCSBN 2024 National Nursing Workforce Study (744,714 RN respondents): male RNs 10% (11% in 2022); median age 50 (46 in 2022); hospitals primary setting for 53% of RNs; median pretax annual earnings $88,000 (2024; $80,000 in 2022); 20% changed settings; 40% plan to leave the profession within 5 years. The page does NOT report female share, night/rotating share or travel/per-diem share — [NCSBN 2024 RN highlights](https://ncsbn.org/research/recent-research/workforce/2024-workforce-rn.page), fetched 2026-10-27.
- Male share 10.4% in 2024 (11.2% in 2022; 8% in 2015); a secondary summary lists female 89.3%, nonbinary 0.4% — [Becker's Hospital Review](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/workforce/after-years-of-gains-male-rn-representation-fell-to-10-4-5-notes.html), search-summary only, UNVERIFIED on the 89.3% figure.
- 87.7% of RNs actively employed in nursing; 138,000+ nurses left since 2022 — [NCSBN workforce hub / Business Wire release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250417747669/en/NCSBN-Research-Highlights-Small-Steps-Toward-Nursing-Workforce-Recovery-Burnout-and-Staffing-Challenges-Persist), search-summary only.
- Shift pattern evidence is dated: a 2009 ANA poll of 14,000+ nurses found 59.4% worked 12-hour shifts and 20.7% worked straight 12-hour nights (cited in a CDC document) — [CDC stacks PDF](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/191903/cdc_191903_DS1.pdf), search-summary only; UNVERIFIED and 17 years old.
- A NYU survey of 4,500+ newly licensed nurses in 13 states and DC: most work 12-hour shifts, 13% hold a second job, nearly half work overtime (publication date unclear) — [Daily Nurse](https://dailynurse.springerpub.com/careers-and-recruitment/nyu-nursing-reports-new-nurses-work-overtime-long-shifts-and-sometimes-a-second-job/), search-summary only, UNVERIFIED.
- 12-hour shifts: many staff value the off-duty days and lower travel and childcare costs, though they may raise burnout and safety risk (2025 Nursing Management article) — [EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/articles/business-and-management/41658b4a-a8f3-57b6-8475-2ab289234727/12-hour-nursing-shifts-are-they-helpful-or-harmful-to-staff-12-hour-shifts-may-increase-risk-of-nurse-burnout-and-compromise-patient-safety-but-many-staff-value-the-off-duty-days-and-lower-travel-and-childcare-costs), search-summary only.
- Travel nurse contracts are typically 13 weeks (general explainer, no percentages) — [advancestudy.org](https://advancestudy.org/?p=425302), UNVERIFIED blog.

### Inferences
- Core buyer is a woman in her 40s-50s on average (younger on nights is plausible but not sourced); this means the brand's voice should not default to a Gen Z register, which also helps with the no-minors rule.
- Mid-to-high income ($88K-$97K median) suggests price sensitivity is about per-use value and subscriptions fatigue, not affordability; not tested.

### Gaps
- Share of RNs on nights/rotating shifts; share travel vs staff vs per-diem; age bands; pay by setting (hospital vs ambulatory vs nursing home) and night differentials; commute length and break patterns. NSSRN, AACN and ANA figures were not retrievable. Next step: open the NCSBN Journal of Nursing Regulation article (Smiley et al., 2025) and the BLS OEWS by-industry table directly.

## Caffeine habits

### Takeaway
Caffeine use is near-universal among studied nurses (coffee dominant, energy drinks a minority) and breaks are scarce and often used for eating. No peer-reviewed data on how nurses buy caffeine or on pouches was found.

### Cited Findings
- Caffeine use among nurses: 88.1% (US), 90.8% (Italy), 92% (Korea), n=182; US daily energy-drink use 11.9% vs 64% in Korea; over half of Korean and US nurses said colleagues drink them at work — Phillips et al. 2021, [PMC table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9023427/table/Tab1) and search summary; abstract not fully read.
- 476 nurses: 22.5% energy-drink consumers, 62.8% caffeine-only, 14.7% none; energy-drink users reported poorer sleep and higher stress (association) — Higbee 2020, [Acta Biomedica](https://mattioli1885journals.com/index.php/actabiomedica/article/view/15651), search summary.
- 22 Canadian night-shift nurses averaged 2.69 on a 1-5 caffeine-intake scale (pilot) — Okechukwu 2023, search summary only; UNVERIFIED.
- Longitudinal nurse study: caffeine use rose across all schedule groups including night workers, differences between groups not significant — [Frontiers in Public Health 2019](https://doaj.org/article/656b787ecf934de999bf92cbdb741f75), search summary.
- Healthcare workers' night-shift energy-drink use is described as a possible at-risk category — Farinetti 2024, [Addiction](https://iris.unimore.it/bitstream/11380/1330532/1/Addiction%20-%202024%20-%20Farinetti%20-%20Health%E2%80%90care%20personnel%20and%20energy%20drinks%20%20A%20new%20category%20of%20subjects%20at%20risk.pdf), search summary (Italy-focused).
- Night-shift breaks: qualitative study themes were breaks as time to eat, inconsistent unit support, and "a luxury, not a right"; nurses described "an absence of consistent and restorative breaks" — [Workplace Health Saf, Jan 2021 (Providence)](https://digitalcommons.providence.org/publications/4391), fetched 2026-10-27; sample size not shown in fetched text.
- 806 12-hour-shift nurses (Univ. of Tennessee, 2023): most did not take regular rest breaks; breaks often cut short — [OR Manager brief](https://www.ormanager.com/briefs/rest-break-practices-of-12-hour-shift-nurses/), search summary, UNVERIFIED detail.
- A Becker's roundup of nurse Reddit advice includes a comment that some colleagues take about 500 mg per shift and report headaches, poor sleep and anxiety — [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/quality/8-self-care-tips-nurses-wish-they-knew-sooner/), search-summary only (page returned 403); UNVERIFIED and not an exact quote.

### Inferences
- Habit-level fit: no free hands, scarce and interrupted breaks, and a closed cafeteria at night make a no-cup, pocketable format a convenience story, expressed as format and logistics only, never as alertness.
- The 500 mg-per-shift anecdote shows peers already police excess; daily-total clarity on the label suits this audience and fits the 300 mg at 12 pouches issue in CLAUDE.md.

### Gaps
- Servings per shift, timing of intake, dislikes (stomach, jitters, bathroom trips, dental staining), cafeteria-closed evidence, and purchase channels (Amazon, vending, convenience, Costco) for nurses: no credible source found. Stomach, jitters, bathroom and staining complaints are plausible but UNSOURCED here; do not use as claims.

### Cited Findings

## Women-specific caffeine guidance (for counsel; no claims drafted)

### Takeaway
FDA gives 400 mg a day for most adults and declines to set a limit for pregnant or breastfeeding women, telling them to ask a health care provider; the 200 mg (pregnancy) figure attributed to ACOG could not be confirmed from ACOG directly.

### Cited Findings
- FDA: "For most adults, the FDA has cited 400 milligrams a day" as not generally associated with negative effects; if pregnant, trying to become pregnant or breastfeeding, "talk to your health care provider" — [FDA, Spilling the Beans](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much), fetched 2026-10-27.
- ACOG is widely reported as advising under 200 mg a day in pregnancy; ACOG page returned HTTP 402, so wording unconfirmed — [ACOG FAQ](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/moderate-caffeine-consumption-during-pregnancy), UNVERIFIED (secondary: [Doctronic](https://www.doctronic.ai/blog/how-much-caffeine-is-safe-during-pregnancy/)).
- A National Academies slide deck gives under 200 mg/day for pregnancy and 300 mg/day for lactation, lower for newborn or preterm infants — [National Academies PDF](https://nationalacademies.org/event/01-29-2020/docs/DAD98AEED6500F1587EC30594D568B0901B571C912FE?noSaveAs=1), search summary, UNVERIFIED. Health Canada and the UK FSA figures (300 mg / 200 mg) are non-US and out of scope.
- CDC breastfeeding caffeine page: the URL tried returned 404; not retrieved — [CDC attempted URL](https://www.cdc.gov/breastfeeding-special-circumstances/hcp/diet-micronutrients/caffeine.html).
- Roon's own page carries "not recommended for pregnant or lactating people" wording — [Roon](https://www.takeroon.com/pouches/nurses-night-shift-focus), UNVERIFIED competitor precedent.

### Inferences
- Because most buyers are women with an average age of 50 but many are of childbearing age, counsel should decide label and website treatment; this is flagged below, not resolved.

### Gaps
- ACOG and CDC primary text; what a 25 mg label must or should say about pregnancy/lactation (FDA labeling guidance is under consideration per CLAUDE.md).
