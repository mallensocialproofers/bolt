# Coffee drinkers lead, but no niche is proven yet

**The evidence does not support a single clear winner, and no niche can be called profitable on current data.** The least-bad first niche is adult coffee drinkers who want a portable, spill-free, taste-led alternative to a cup, reached through search content, creators and bundled Shopify offers, with claim-free messaging limited to format, price, flavor and 21+. The runner-up is road and shift workers (truckers first), sold on format and price only, never on fatigue or alertness. The test cell is golf, with hunters/anglers as the alternate. Five niches score within one point of each other on the table below (coffee 25, golf 25, remote/knowledge workers 25, hunters/anglers 24, truckers 22), which is noise, because every score rests on size and policy facts rather than on any CAC, repeat-purchase or per-niche COGS data, and none of that data exists in the notes. The unit-economics model is niche-agnostic: **AOV per order, not dose per pouch, decides profitability**. A one-tin order loses money at the assumed cost in every scenario, and a 3-tin first order is roughly break-even on first-order contribution at best. The 25 mg strength is also the weakest part of the price story, at about $1.32 per 100 mg against $0.28 to $0.60 for 100-200 mg rivals. Per CLAUDE.md, the minimum age is treated as 21+ because `work/decisions.md` has no recorded decision, and nothing here recommends spending before the owner clears the compliance gate. Source checks were all made on 2026-10-27.

## The recommendation, runner-up and test cell, with the conditions that decide each

**First niche: coffee drinkers, framed as a format.** The case rests on three things. It is the only pool that is adult by definition and large enough that a cheap test will produce signal quickly. It is the only pool where claim-free copy (taste, portability, no spill, price per pouch, "caffeine, no nicotine, 21+") is the natural message rather than a constrained one. And it can be reached without relying on paid-social acceptance, which is unresolved: no platform wording naming caffeine pouches was found, Meta requires 18+ targeting for supplement ads, and whether a caffeine pouch counts as a supplement there is unresolved ([Meta Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/content-specific-restrictions/cosmetic-procedures-and-wellness)). Amazon search volume is the one quantitative demand signal: "caffeine pouches" at about 30,600 searches a month, with the NZE brand term at about 53,100 (modelled estimate, snapshot date not shown; [MerchantWords](https://www.merchantwords.com/search/us/caffeine)). Coffee drinkers win only if three conditions hold: counsel clears the sensory and convenience wording; an organic or creator test lands first-order CAC under the break-even for a 3-tin bundle (see below); and the 25 mg low-dose story is not drowned by the broader field. The weakness is differentiation: this is the most crowded message, Grinds already sells coffee pouches at 25 mg for about $5.95 ([Roon cheapest-per-serving](https://www.takeroon.com/blog/cheapest-nootropic-pouches-per-serving-compared), UNVERIFIED), and the broad audience means the highest CAC risk. Remote and knowledge workers are best treated as a message segment inside this niche, not a separate market, because no caffeine-habit data for them was found.

**Runner-up: truckers and road workers, format and price only.** The case is real: 2.2M jobs, BLS notes nights, weekends and holidays are common, and fuel stops are where pouches are already sold, with Wip announced in 330+ Walmart Fuel Centers on June 15, 2026 and Grinds claiming 17 chains including Love's, Pilot and Murphy USA ([Nosh](https://www.nosh.com/pr/2026/06/15/wip-energy-pouches-now-available-in-over-330-walmart-fuel-centers-nationwide), [Roon gas stations](https://www.takeroon.com/blog/what-gas-stations-sell-caffeine-pouches); both brand or competitor claims, UNVERIFIED). No brand found names truckers in its own copy. Truckers win if an independent truck-stop or distributor call shows a third pouch brand can get on shelf, and if a lawyer approves wording that mentions the road without implying alertness. The same shelf is held by two scaled competitors at 100-200 mg, and the natural story (staying awake) is exactly what the rules forbid, so this is a conditional runner-up.

**Test cell: golf, with hunters/anglers as the alternate.** Golf has the cleanest profile on the table: no pouch brand found, an older and affluent skew, and controlled channels such as golf media; a 2026 LINKS media kit reports household income of $253,000 and median age 55, which is the publisher's own claim ([LINKS](https://linksmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026-LINKS-Media-Kit.V4.pdf), UNVERIFIED). It is a test cell, not the lead, because pouch fit is unproven and the audience may simply prefer coffee on the course. Golf wins if a small paid or sponsored placement produces waitlist sign-ups at a CAC that beats the coffee test. Hunters and anglers are the bigger pool (40M anglers, 14.4M hunters) and already buy through outfitters, but the natural hook is replacing dip, which the rules prohibit as a nicotine-replacement implication. Grinds began as a chew alternative and BaccOff is sold through a hunting publication ([Buckmasters](https://buckmasters.com/Hunting/Gear-For-Deer/Type/View/Product/1280), UNVERIFIED), so only a convenience framing (a pouch for the stand or the boat) is available, and that overlaps what Grinds already does there.

**What would change the ranking.** The notes name the real unknowns: actual CAC by niche, pouch repeat rates, whether ad platforms accept caffeine pouch ads at all, whether hospitals, bases or employers restrict oral pouches, and whether Love's or Pilot category managers would add a third brand. Any one of these can reorder the top five.

## The model is niche-agnostic, and AOV decides everything

The model (`unit_economics.py`, rerunnable) takes the niche in only through price, AOV and CAC inputs, and no niche-specific price point or CAC could be sourced. Its first-order contribution is AOV minus COGS, card fees, a refund allowance, shipping and pick-pack. In the mid case ($8.99 per tin, COGS $2.50, shipping $5.50) a **one-tin order loses $2.25**, and it loses money in nearly every price and COGS scenario, so a single-tin first order cannot be the offer. Two tins earn $3.80, three tins $9.85 and five tins $21.95. Break-even CAC over a year at 8% monthly churn for a 3-tin order is $75.99 at the lowest usage scenario (3 pouches a day) and rises with usage only because reorders come faster; that is arithmetic, not a goal, and the model's higher-use rows exist only to show sensitivity. Heavy daily use is not an objective: the category itself caps use (Ultra says maximum two a day, Nectr about eight at 50 mg), and FDA's reference ceiling is about 400 mg a day from all sources, or 16 pouches at 25 mg ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much)). Against vendor CAC ranges of roughly $40-$120 (generic DTC and supplements, not pouches), a 3-tin buyer at 3 pouches a day pays back in 60, 120 or 240 days at CAC $25, $40 or $60, and a $120 CAC does not pay back inside the year. At a generic Meta ROAS near 3, implied first-order CAC is AOV/3, about $9 on a $26.97 order, which roughly matches first-order contribution; the two CAC views conflict, which is itself a finding.

Three price facts shape the offer. At $5.99 per tin (the Grinds and Wip price point), 1-3 tin orders are negative or near zero (three tins earn $1.29), and five tins earn $7.68. At $10.99 two tins earn $7.60. Shipping at $8.00 instead of $5.50 cuts first-order contribution by $2.50 per order. And the model holds shipping flat across 1-5 tins, which is optimistic because tin weight is unsourced and larger orders likely exceed 4 oz. The **25 mg price-per-100 mg disadvantage** is structural: a 25 mg pouch at $0.33 to $0.45 costs $1.32 to $1.80 per 100 mg, above every 100-200 mg competitor (Wip 200 mg $0.28, Wip 100 mg $0.49, Mojo 100 mg $0.60, Nectr single tins $1.10 to $1.20, Grinds 25 mg $1.32; see `unit_economics_competitors.csv`, built from Roon tables, UNVERIFIED). The pitch therefore has to be per pouch and per tin, not per milligram, and margin has to come from multipacks and low COGS.

**Wholesale is thinner and less proven.** No published wholesale terms were found. Using unsourced vendor ranges (distributor 15-35%, retailer markup 38-45% on cost; [mrcheckout.net](https://mrcheckout.net/convenience-store-distribution-faq/), UNVERIFIED) and assumed retailer margins of 28-40%, brand net per tin at a $5.99 MSRP runs from $4.31 direct to $2.34 through a distributor at the high end. At $5.99, wholesale works only if COGS is at or below about $2.50 and the distributor layer is thin or absent; at $8.99 contribution stays positive in nearly every cell except COGS $3.50 with 40% retailer and 35% distributor margins (about -$0.24). Wholesale has zero CAC and no shipping, but each retailer or distributor ask depends on slotting and terms that are unknown; the only slotting figure ($100 low, $500 average, $2,000 high per SKU per store) comes from an unsourced skill page ([Smithery](https://smithery.ai/skills/kishorkukreja/slotting-fees-optimization), UNVERIFIED).

**Every assumption with no source, listed in full.** COGS of $1.50, $2.50 or $3.50 per tin (the only cost hint, a Sourcify FOB range of $0.78-$2.60 with undefined unit, is unreliable; even caffeine anhydrous at lab-reagent $455/kg is only about $0.23 per 20-pouch tin, so filler, fleece, flavor, tin and labor dominate). Twenty pouches per tin (competitors use 10, 15, 18 or 20). Pick-pack of $2.50. A 2% refund and chargeback allowance. Flat shipping at $5.50 or $8.00 regardless of tin count, with no adult-signature fee (third-party estimates for adult signature are about $9-$11 per package). A 15% subscription discount (a Roon-reported Wip figure, UNVERIFIED; Nectr's own site says 35%). CAC test points of $25, $40 and $60. A $0.25 per tin wholesale freight cost and the retailer and distributor splits. No fixed costs, though 25,000 cans at the assumed COGS would be $37.5k-$87.5k of inventory (the 25,000-can MOQ could not be confirmed), plus $700-$4,500 a year for product liability by broker estimates ([Assureful](https://www.assureful.com/blog/how-much-does-product-liability-insurance-cost-data-driven-pricing-stats-you-need), UNVERIFIED). Sourced inputs are limited to price anchors, the 2.9% + $0.30 card fee ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/pricing)), published churn ranges from vendor blogs (5-8% to 8-12% monthly, with 60-70% lost between orders 1 and 3; [Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/supplement-subscription-churn-rate-benchmark), UNVERIFIED), and generic Meta ROAS ([Top Growth Marketing](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/dtc-ecommerce-benchmarks/meta-ads-benchmarks/)). Q4 is also the most expensive ad window, so tests run now would see inflated CPMs.

## The claim-risk trap and the youth climate

Fatigue and alertness framing for truckers, nurses or pilots, and dip-replacement framing for hunters, brush against the Bolt rules even when the copy never uses a banned word. The FTC's health products guidance says claims must be substantiated "expressly or by implication," that the net impression of an ad governs, and that product names and imagery can imply a claim; anecdotes are never enough ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance)). Names like "Wide Awake" or "Night Shift" therefore carry implied-claim risk. For drivers, fatigue is also a regulated safety topic through hours-of-service limits (11 hours driving in a 14-hour window; BLS), so a "stay awake behind the wheel" message reads as fatigue-masking, not just a performance claim. The workaround is narrow: **only format, price, flavor and 21+ messaging is claim-free.** That means an occupational niche can be targeted by channel (an independent truck stop, a trade publication, a creator in the trade) but the creative must say nothing about why the buyer is tired. A cautionary data point: 79% of surveyed truckers still felt tired after caffeine in one trade survey (search summary, UNVERIFIED), a finding that must not appear in any Bolt copy.

The youth and look-alike climate is worsening right now. AP reporting carried by Fortune on 2026-10-22 describes school districts in New York, Kansas, Michigan, Wisconsin, Pennsylvania and Missouri banning or restricting caffeine pouches, staff unable to tell them from nicotine pouches, the American Academy of Pediatrics noting some products with up to 200 mg, and a July CDC report that nearly two-thirds of 12-19-year-olds consume caffeine on a given day; no state laws were mentioned ([Fortune/AP](https://fortune.com/2026/10/22/schools-ban-caffeine-pouches-students-coffee/)). YPulse calls the product a trend "especially among U.S. teens" ([YPulse](https://www.ypulse.com/newsfeed/2026/06/11/gen-z-is-helping-create-a-caffeine-boom/)). There is no federal minimum age for caffeine pouches as of September 2026 (a vaping retailer source, UNVERIFIED; [Vaping360](https://vaping360.com/learn/how-old-to-buy-caffeine-pouches/)), so 21+ is a brand rule that checkout and retailer agreements must enforce; Nectr requires 18+ online and Mojo says no age restriction. Because retailers set their own age policy, shelf placement next to nicotine products and the 21+ gate are written-agreement questions. Gaming, gym and campus-adjacent grad-student framings combine the highest youth exposure with the highest claim exposure, which is why they score lowest. Packaging that does not mimic nicotine tins and states "caffeine, no nicotine, 21+" would lower look-alike risk, but that is a design inference, not a sourced finding.

## A no-spend validation plan, in order

Nothing below spends money, publishes, or contacts anyone on Bolt's behalf, and none of it starts until the owner records the compliance gate as cleared in `work/decisions.md`; the owner approves every outbound step. The sequence is as follows.

1. **Desk research (free, first).** Read Meta, Google, TikTok, YouTube, Reddit and Twitch ad policy text directly, and read the NCA, ESA, NGF and USFWS primaries to replace search summaries. Record which niches' ad channels are open to a caffeine pouch at all, since this single unknown can reorder the table.
2. **Manual Meta Ad Library check.** Search Nectr, Roon, Wip, Mojo, Ultra and Grinds by hand. Whether competitors run paid ads, and for which niche creative, was not retrievable and is the cheapest evidence still missing.
3. **Manual Reddit and creator checks.** Read the rules and tone of the relevant subreddits (trucking, nursing, golf, hunting and fishing, coffee) before any participation, since the notes found none of the actual rules; count creators who already review pouches in each niche and check their audiences' age.
4. **Retailer and distributor calls (owner-approved scripts).** Ask one independent truck stop group, one outfitter and one regional distributor for category-review steps, margin, slotting and any 25 mg objection. These calls decide the truckers runner-up and the hunters alternate and produce the wholesale terms the model lacks.
5. **Co-packer quote.** One real COGS quote per tin collapses the biggest single model assumption.
6. **Landing-page waitlist (after the compliance gate and a counsel-approved wording review).** One 21+ gated page per niche message (coffee format, golf, trucker format) with a 3-tin bundle as the only offer; the metric is waitlist cost per sign-up and bundle preference. Ad spend, if any, only after the owner approves it, and only against the break-even CAC for the bundle.

A decision for the owner (to add to `work/decisions.md` per the project rules): approve coffee drinkers as the lead message for desk and waitlist testing, hold truckers and golf as parallel tests, and require a 3-tin minimum first offer.

## For a professional

**Lawyer.** Wording for occupational niches: whether any copy that mentions truckers, nurses or night work can avoid an implied fatigue or alertness claim under the FTC standard, and whether names such as "Night Shift" are acceptable. Workplace, DOT and hospital issues: whether caffeine pouch use or marketing raises employer, FMCSA or hospital-policy problems (the 49 CFR 40.82 panel text was not retrieved, so the DOT-testing point rests on general knowledge, not a read primary). Hunters and golf: whether a "pouch for the stand" message can avoid a nicotine-replacement implication. The 21+ gate: how to enforce it at checkout, in wholesale terms and on shelf, given no federal minimum and school-ban momentum, and whether state laws have changed since the AP coverage. Supplement versus food status: how ad platforms classify a caffeine pouch (Meta's supplement rule needs 18+ targeting; classification was unresolved), and whether sensory wording such as "no acid" or "no staining" is acceptable. Trademark clearance of Increment or Hasp before any niche-specific branding.

**Accountant.** Whether a one-tin loss-leader first order, shipping subsidies and bundle pricing are sensible given the model's assumptions; the reality of chargeback monitoring thresholds (reported as 1.5% for Visa from April 2026 in one source and 0.9% in another); inventory exposure on a first run of about 25,000 cans; sales-tax nexus across states.

**Insurance broker.** Product liability for a 25 mg caffeine pouch (broker estimates run $700-$4,500 a year, UNVERIFIED), how a sold-to-minors or school-incident scenario affects cover, and whether wholesale retailers will demand certificates of insurance.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. Which niche is largest was never in doubt, since coffee drinkers dwarf everyone, but the largest niche is also the least differentiated, and the niches with an obvious emotional hook (tired truckers, night nurses, dip quitters) are exactly where the rules forbid the hook. The practical choice is therefore between a broad claim-free format message and a narrow channel play, and the model says both fail at one-tin orders. The first real decision is the offer: a 3-tin bundle at a price that survives $5.50 to $8.00 shipping, sold to a niche cheap enough to reach to cover a CAC that is currently unknown.

What the evidence cannot say is as important as what it can. No competitor's customer data, no pouch CAC and no niche repeat rate were found, and the competitor-authored sources (Roon above all) supply much of the pricing and positioning evidence. Until the manual ad-library, retailer and co-packer checks are done, treat the recommendation as a testing order, not a market verdict.
