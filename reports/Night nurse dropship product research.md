# Test socks first, planner second, snacks last

**Yes, there are alternatives to caffeine pouches that a Shopify brand could sell as a subscription to US women nurses on overnight shifts, but the evidence is thin and the first step is a cheap test, not a launch.** The model ranks three concepts: (1) quarterly socks from a small-minimum-order importer, which earn a base **$38.73** contribution per customer over 12 months (range $25.51 to $61.60); (2) a digital shift-planner membership, base **$50.17** (range $40.55 to $58.35), the cheapest way to test whether the audience pays at all; and (3) a snack-and-tea box, base **$55.89** but with a very wide range ($14.19 to $109.58), so it runs only as a limited pre-sold batch. Nine other concepts lose money or barely earn at the tested prices and should be dropped for now. Treat every number as provisional. Supplier costs are mostly benchmark-derived (50%, 75% or 100% of a retail price), not quoted. Every subscription price is the researcher's assumption inside a sourced retail band. There is no sourced refund, repeat or willingness-to-pay data, and most primary pages failed to load, so the figures rest on search snippets and vendor blogs. The one structural finding that does hold up is arithmetic: **$5.50 to $8.40 of postage plus $2.50 pick/pack consumes a $13 to $20 price**, so a physical subscription needs roughly $30 or more per shipment, or a 90-day cadence, to stay positive. Nothing here is a legal conclusion, and the compliance gate in CLAUDE.md still governs any spend.

## Three concepts clear the bar, and each wins only under stated conditions

The recommendation comes from a single contribution model (price, minus item cost, card fee of 2.9% plus $0.30, 2% refunds, shipping, packaging and $2.50 pick/pack, summed over 12 months with monthly churn) built in `subscription_economics_concepts.py`. It reproduces the earlier Delphine pouch model's $12.09 first-order contribution as a check. "Break-even CAC" below means the 12-month contribution per customer before fixed costs; it is the most you can pay to acquire a customer and still break even. The earlier Delphine finance model used a $25 hard ceiling and a $40 fail line for CAC. Against those lines, only the snack box and the digital membership clear $40 at base inputs, and no concept clears $60.

**First test: quarterly compression-style socks via a small-MOQ importer (concept 1b).** The model sells three pairs every 90 days at an assumed $34.99. It earns **$13.56 per order (38.7% of price)** and $38.73 over a year at 8% monthly churn. This is the best physical concept for three reasons: it is the only category with a sourced replacement cadence (3 to 6 months per retailer and podiatry blogs, UNVERIFIED), it carries the strongest nurse demand signal in the research (third-party trackers estimate roughly 10,000 Amazon units a month for several women's nurse sock listings, also UNVERIFIED), and a higher-ticket quarterly parcel absorbs shipping better than a monthly one. It wins only if all of the following hold. The landed cost per pair stays at or below the break-even point of about **$7.51 per pair**, which is where contribution per order hits zero (non-item costs are $12.46 per order at $34.99, so three pairs at $7.51 consume the remaining $22.53). At $5.50 landed per pair the order earns $6.03. A 20 to 30 unit pilot shows acceptable delivery time and size-return rate. Counsel is comfortable with the wording, because compression language can pull a sock toward medical-device status (covered below). The model's item cost comes from eBay retail prices of $1.62, $2.99 and $3.51 per pair, used as a proxy for landed cost. Those are not wholesale quotes and say nothing about accuracy of compression, sizing, quality or lead time ([eBay six-pair listing](https://www.ebay.de/itm/406168057260), search snippet, SRC-U, checked 2026-10-27).

**Second test: a digital shift-planner membership (concept 8).** With zero item cost and no shipping, it earns $6.35 per $6.99 order (90.8%), and the model shows $58.35 / $50.17 / $40.55 at 5% / 8% / 12.5% churn. It wins only if people will pay recurring money for something that nurse planners currently sell one time for **$2.99, $5.99, $7 and $9.90** ([Payhip](https://payhip.com/b/fzNM1), [Gumroad](https://fowziee.gumroad.com/l/clinical-shift-starter-kit), snippets, SRC-U, 2026-10-27). The $6.99 recurring price is an assumption with no recurring benchmark behind it, and the model excludes the author's time, hosting or community-platform fees, and ongoing content labor. It fails if buyers only convert one time at $3 to $10. Its value is as an audience and willingness-to-pay test, and as an add-on that could ride along with sock shipments.

**Third test: a snack-and-tea box, only as a limited pre-sold batch (concept 2).** At $29.99 per month it shows the highest base contribution ($7.07 per order, $55.89 over a year) and the widest range. At 50% of retail cost and 5% churn it reaches $109.58; at 100% of retail cost and 12.5% churn it falls to $14.19, and at 100% of retail the contribution is only **$2.22 per order**. It therefore needs real wholesale pricing at about half of retail or better, a 3PL kitting quote, and a box under 16 oz (an assumption). Wholesale prices on Faire are login-gated, so this concept has no quoted cost at all. Curated boxes also churn at 10% to 15% monthly in the one vendor source found ([Churnkey](https://churnkey.co/blog/the-average-churn-rate-for-subscription-services/), SRC-U), which is why the 12.5% scenario is the realistic stress case.

**What to drop for now.** Hand and lip care (base -$21.08 over 12 months), earplug and mask refills (-$19.84), white-label electrolytes (-$10.59, with an unsourced $0.60 stick cost placeholder and an unknown minimum order), the scrunchie club (-$34.69), and the mixed self-care box (-$46.61) all lose money at base cost. The POD scrub cap earns only **$2.95 per order and $8.42 over a year**, which would never cover a $99 per month subscription app. POD comfort socks (concept 1a) earn $22.19 in the base case but fall to **-$50.41** if the supplier is the $23.36 embroidered sock instead of a $7.14 to $9.06 printed one, so keep them as a fallback only if no importer quote is acceptable.

## Postage and pick/pack eat the margin, and dropship subscriptions fail in predictable ways

**Postage is the reason most concepts fail.** USPS Ground Advantage commercial rates for a parcel under 1 lb run **$5.50 (4 oz, near zone) to $8.40 (15.999 oz, zone 8)**, and $7.61 to $10.67 at 1 lb, per a Pirate Ship rate card dated 30 June 2026 reported by Ship.com ([Ship.com, July 15 2026](https://www.ship.com/post/cheapest-way-to-ship-a-package-2026), SRC-U, third-party reseller article). Another search summary puts a retail 1 lb zone 5 rate at $10.95 and commercial at $8.74, plus an 8% fuel surcharge from April 26, 2026 to January 17, 2027 ([Shippo](https://goshippo.com/blog/usps-parcel-select-ground-cost-sizes-and-how-it-works), search summary, SRC-U); the two sources do not fully agree and the surcharge may not be inside the $5.50 to $10.67 range. Add $2.50 pick/pack (an assumption inherited from the Delphine cost model; no 3PL quote), $1.00 to $1.50 packaging (assumed), 2% refunds (assumed) and the 2.9% plus $0.30 card fee, and a $14.99 box has a contribution of -$3.00 on reorders before the item has been paid for. The break-even structure is simple: a physical subscription needs roughly **$30 or more per shipment, or a 90-day cadence**, to leave a positive contribution. Every concept priced at $12.99 to $19.99 failed at base. This is arithmetic on the shipping and fee inputs, not a market finding, and the practical ceiling on price is the market: TheraBox sells at $34.99 to $39.99, Sock Fancy at about $9 plus $2 shipping per pair on a Nov 2023 listing ([My Subscription Addiction](https://www.mysubscriptionaddiction.com/self-care-subscription-boxes?msa=hp); [Sock Fancy listing](https://www.mysubscriptionaddiction.com/b/sock-fancy), both SRC-U). The weights are also an assumption (boxes under 16 oz), and the USPS 2 lb rate was not found.

**Fulfillment options and their limits.** Printful has no order minimum and offers packing inserts, but custom packaging requires stocking inventory in its warehouse ([Printful support](https://support.printful.com/hc/en-us/articles/41396648644497-What-are-Printful-s-shipping-options), checked 2026-10-27). Its US sock shipping is **$4.69 for the first item plus $2.00 each additional**, with a $0.40 surcharge on US orders from October 15, 2026 to January 17, 2027, and custom socks run from $9.06 to $24.94 with no minimum ([Printful shipping](https://www.printful.com/shipping); [Printful socks](https://www.printful.com/custom-socks), both checked 2026-10-27); the sock page shows a $7.14 sublimation figure in its data that conflicts with the $9.06 "from" price, and these are not graduated-compression garments by my reading of the product list. CJ Dropshipping stocks overseas warehouses only at 10 pieces per variant and 100 in total, and its US-warehouse delivery claims conflict (2 to 3, 3 to 6 and 4 to 10 days, with China-origin 7 to 22 days); complaints cluster on variable shipping, a US warehouse lacking the exact variant, uneven support and costly express shipping on low-ticket items ([CJ blog](https://blog.cjdropshipping.cn/detail/Unlock-3-Day-Delivery-with-CJ-s-New-US-Warehouses); [Spocket CJ overview](https://www.spocket.co/blogs/cjdropshipping-overview), vendor-authored on a competitor, UNVERIFIED). CJ's own page redirected to bot validation, so no CJ price was retrieved. Spocket plans run $39.99 (Starter, 25 unique products), $59.99, $99.99 and $299.99 a month, all listing branded invoices, but its pricing page states no shipping times or sample policy ([Spocket pricing](https://www.spocket.co/pricing), checked 2026-10-27). Zendrop has a free tier plus paid plans, with review sources disagreeing on which tiers include branding (UNVERIFIED). Among 3PLs, ShipBob and ShipMonk publish no full rate cards, reported minimums are monthly fees of about $250 to $275 rather than unit minimums, ShipBob onboarding is reported at 4 to 8 weeks, and Fulfillrite is reported from $2.85 per order with a 100 orders per month minimum (all third-party, UNVERIFIED: [Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/shipbob-vs-shipstation-3pl), [fulfill.com](https://www.fulfill.com/shipbob-vs-shipmonk), [Dupple](https://dupple.com/reviews/shipbob)). Under about 100 orders a month a 3PL's minimums and onboarding probably cost more than self-fulfilling, which is an inference. Faire brands set their own first-order and reorder minimums, with lip balm minimums of $50 to $150 and eye mask minimums of $100 to $250 seen on listings ([Faire help](https://www.faire.com/en/support/articles/29090131049371); [Faire CALA](https://www.faire.com/product/p_w942jzjhhv), SRC-U). Private label minimums reported by vendors run 500 to 5,000 units per supplement SKU (some near 100) and 10 gallons for stock skin-care formulas ([Creme de Mint](https://cremedemint.com/blog/industry/herbs-supplements/low-moq-private-label-supplements); [RainShadow Labs](https://www.rainshadowlabs.com/pages/private-label), vendor self-reports, UNVERIFIED), and nothing was found for tea or socks. Amazon Multi-Channel Fulfillment ships in unbranded packaging by default and raised fees in 2026 per third-party blogs, so it is a backup, not a plan.

Loop Starter lists $99 per month plus 1.0% with no per-order fee and a free plan up to 50 subscribers ([Loop pricing](https://loopwork.co/pricing), vendor page). Another source gives Loop at 0.75%, so that is also unsettled. Appstle and Seal are described as having no transaction fee (vendor blogs, UNVERIFIED). The model therefore uses the $0 Shopify app as base and shows Recharge and Loop as sensitivities; at base cost, covering Recharge's $99 monthly fee takes 15 snack-box subscribers, 16 digital subscribers, 22 sock subscribers, and 39 POD-sock subscribers.

**Failure modes to design for.** Dropship and POD subscriptions fail through listing removal, supplier price changes, slow or variable shipping, unbranded parcels, variant mismatches and quality drift that cannot be seen from a listing, and margin squeeze when a subscription discount (15% in the pouch model) meets processing fees. No source gave delisting or price-change frequencies, so these are design risks and not measured rates (UNVERIFIED). The remedy in the notes is to order samples of the exact variant from the US warehouse and re-sample on a schedule. Payments add a further risk: Shopify Payments is subject to onboarding, underwriting and a prohibited and restricted list, may impose reserves, and its reserves help page names extended billing cycles such as annual subscriptions as especially exposed to disputes ([Shopify Payments terms](https://www.shopify.com/legal/terms-payfac); [Shopify reserves help](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/shopify-payments/reserves)); reports of 10% to 20% reserves on dropshipping stores are anecdotal forum posts (UNVERIFIED).

## Subscription law applies before any product is chosen

None of this is legal advice and none of it concludes that any flow is compliant; it is a list of rules for counsel to apply.

**Federal.** The Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act (ROSCA) requires online sellers of negative-option offers to disclose all material terms clearly before collecting billing information, obtain express informed consent before charging, and provide simple mechanisms to stop recurring charges ([Cornell LII, 15 USC 8403](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/8403), medium confidence, mirror of statute). The FTC's Mail, Internet or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule requires a reasonable basis to ship within the advertised time, or within 30 days if none is stated, and consent or refund for delays; the page read does not carve out dropshippers ([FTC rule page](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/mail-internet-or-telephone-order-merchandise-rule), checked 2026-10-27). Overseas dropship lead times of 7 to 22 days fit inside 30 days but leave little margin, so a stated ship-by time on the product page matters (inference). The FTC's amended "click-to-cancel" rule was vacated by the Eighth Circuit on **July 8, 2025** ([Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/eighth-circuit-vacates-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule), law firm). The compliance note reports the FTC restored the Negative Option Rule to its pre-2024 form on February 12, 2026 and published a new advance notice of proposed rulemaking on March 13, 2026 with comments due April 13, 2026; ROSCA remains enforceable, and no later proposed rule was found ([Federal Register, Feb 12 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/12/2026-02866/revision-of-the-negative-option-rule-withdrawal-of-the-cars-rule-removal-of-the-non-compete-rule-to); [Federal Register, Mar 13 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/03/13/2026-04952/rule-concerning-the-use-of-prenotification-negative-option-plans); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/ftc-restarts-negative-option-rulemaking-after-eighth-circuit-vacatur-enforcement-under-rosca-continues/), summaries of the pages, not read in full).

**States.** California's automatic renewal amendments (AB 2863, effective July 1, 2025) are reported to require express affirmative consent to renewal terms, cover free-to-paid conversions, require annual reminders, and require cancellation in the same medium used to sign up; these are law-firm summaries only, because the statute page returned 403 ([Barnes & Thornburg](https://btlaw.com/en/insights/alerts/2025/california-expands-automatic-renewal-law-new-requirements-now-in-effect); [Fenwick](https://fenwick.com/insights/publications/california-tightens-requirements-for-automatically-renewing-subscriptions)). The fulfillment note describes the earlier California rules (online sign-up requires online cancellation, plus AB 390 renewal notices) from law-firm summaries too ([Manatt](https://manatt.com/insights/newsletters/advertising-law/ca-renews-auto-renewal-law)). New York's amended law (General Business Law 527 and 527-a), effective November 5, 2025, is reported to require disclosing material terms before collecting billing information, a cancellation mechanism at least as easy as sign-up, and price-increase consent or a 14-day cancel window; pending bills would require online-only cancellation, with enactment unconfirmed ([Ashurst](https://www.ashurstperkinscoie.com/insights/update/new-york-and-colorado-update-auto-renewing-subscription-requirements); [NY Senate S5615](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S5615)). Other states were not researched. The sensible design assumption is the strictest state plus ROSCA: separate unchecked consent, price, term and frequency shown before payment, email confirmation, one-click online cancel, annual reminders and easy skip or pause (inference). **Lawyer flag:** a 50-state auto-renewal review and a compliant cancellation flow belong with counsel before any paid subscription goes live.

## Claim traps decide which categories are even available

**Compression socks.** Under 21 CFR 880.5780 a medical support stocking is "constructed of elastic material and designed to apply controlled pressure to the leg"; it is Class II if intended for preventing blood pooling in the leg and Class I if intended for other medical purposes ([eCFR 880.5780](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-21?part=880&section=880.5780), WebFetch summary, checked 2026-10-27). FDA clearances for flight socks cite indications such as helping prevent edema, leg discomfort and DVT ([FDA K032325](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf3/K032325.pdf); [Innolitics K062873](https://fda.innolitics.com/device/K062873), mirror). Whether a sock with graduated pressure and no health claim is a device cannot be settled from the regulation text, because it turns on intended use. FDA reissued its General Wellness policy on January 6, 2026, but the guidance text was not read and the search result did not address compression products ([FDA town hall page](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/medical-devices-news-and-events/town-hall-refresh-general-wellness-policy-low-risk-devices-02112026)). The practical consequence: the sock concept must be written as fit, pattern, durability and comfort, with no circulation, swelling, DVT or mmHg-as-therapy language, and the demand sources that justify socks by circulation or fatigue cannot be reused in copy. One podiatry page claims a 20 mmHg sock may deliver about 10 mmHg after six months; that is a performance-adjacent claim and must not be repeated. The concept is also a reason to prefer plain "comfort sock" sourcing over graduated-compression sourcing, which is a decision for counsel.

**Cosmetics.** The Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act (MoCRA) requires facility registration, product listing, safety substantiation and 15-business-day serious adverse event reports, with certain small businesses exempt from registration, listing and GMP ([FDA MoCRA page](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-laws-regulations/modernization-cosmetics-regulation-act-2022-mocra), checked 2026-10-27). The small-business test is average annual US cosmetic sales under $1,000,000 over three years, and it excludes products that regularly contact the eye mucous membrane, are injected, are for internal use or alter appearance for more than 24 hours ([Cornell LII, 21 USC 364h](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/364h)). The responsible person is the manufacturer, packer or distributor named on the label, so a brand that puts its name on a dropshipped cream carries adverse-event reporting and safety substantiation even if exempt from registration ([Cornell LII, 21 USC 364](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/364)). One secondary source says FDA issues no exemption certificates, and sources disagreed on dates and on whether listing is exempt (UNVERIFIED).

**Supplements, caffeine and food.** Dietary supplement statements must not be disease claims, need an FDA notification within 30 days and carry the standard disclaimer ([eCFR 21 CFR 101.93](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-21?part=101&section=101.93), WebFetch summary). The text read does not mention sleep or insomnia, and one columnist reports a "sleep aid" label flagged by Amazon (UNVERIFIED). House rule 4 bans sleep, alertness, fatigue and performance claims, which leaves the supplement claim space nearly empty; this is why the compliance note rates supplements and caffeine High and why the electrolyte concept is dropped on both economics and compliance grounds. Conventional foods and tea face food-facility registration, labeling and allergen rules, and the note rates the snack box Medium if it avoids "energy" or "alertness" language and High if positioned as functional or caffeinated ([FDA registration page](https://www.fda.gov/food/guidance-regulation-food-and-dietary-supplements/registration-food-facilities-and-other-submissions), exemptions not confirmed).

**Nurse-targeted marketing.** Under the FTC Endorsement Guides, material connections (payments, free product, affiliate links) must be disclosed clearly, an endorser claiming expertise must have it and exercise it, and brands can be liable for deceptive endorsements ([16 CFR Part 255](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-16?part=255), WebFetch summary, medium confidence). A creator who is a nurse is probably an expert only for what her training covers, and "nurse-approved" or RN credentials implying medical endorsement of a sock or cream is a high-risk phrase (inference). Meta prohibits ads that assert or imply a viewer's physical or mental health condition, so "tired after your night shift?" or "your swollen feet" copy can read as asserting a condition ([Meta personal attributes policy](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/privacy-violations-personal-attributes/), via search summary; page not opened), and Meta's Health and Wellness policy bars appearance and hygiene shaming and exaggerated outcome claims ([Meta Health and Wellness](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness/)). Meta requires supplement and weight-loss ads to target ages 18 and over, while general foods and non-permanent cosmetics are exempt from that requirement. Write about the product, never the viewer's state. Do not use ANA, AACN or ANCC names, logos or "accredited" or "endorsed by" language without written permission, and treat "nurse" or "RN" in a product name as an open trademark question; none of this was researched in primary sources.

**Not researched, so unknown.** 21 CFR Part 111 supplement cGMP, hand sanitizer and heated or medicated patch rules (OTC drug and EPA pathways), earplug noise-reduction-rating labeling, state nursing-board rules on using the RN title in advertising, payment-processor prohibited lists (Stripe, Shopify Payments, PayPal), TikTok and Google Ads health policies, textile and care-label rules, nutrition-facts small-business exemptions, cottage-food laws, and all sales-tax treatment by category and state. The sales-tax research returned nothing, so the accountant question is wide open.

## What the demand research established, and what it did not

The demand evidence is weaker than the economics. **Compression socks have the only sourced reorder cadence**: replace every 3 to 6 months per retailer and podiatrist blogs ([myshoesreview.com](https://myshoesreview.com/?p=43276); [Michigan Foot Doctors](https://www.michiganfootdoctors.com/best-compression-socks-for-nurses/), UNVERIFIED), and one competitor, Herzog Medical, already runs a 6-month auto-ship with a three-delivery minimum ([Herzog Medical](https://herzogmedical.com/en/collections/pro-compression-socks-subscription)). A broker listing for a nurse-focused sock brand says it was considering a monthly subscription "since most people own multiple pairs" (seller-authored, UNVERIFIED, [Website Closers](https://www.websiteclosers.com/businesses/compression-socks-ecommerce-brand-for-nurses-healthcare-workers-140-yoy-growth-all-in-house-designs/104999)). Every other cadence in the longlist (hand cream at 1 to 3 months, earplugs at 1 to 2 months, and so on) is the researcher's reasoning, not data.

**Existing nurse boxes are gift-themed, not consumable restocks.** Nursa's ranking lists ten boxes with tumblers, tees, candles, reusable straws, compression socks and snacks, and gives no prices, reviews or churn; its page is dated 2023 and whether the boxes still operate in October 2026 is unconfirmed ([Nursa](https://nursa.com/blog/10-nursing-subscription-boxes-to-try), vendor-adjacent, UNVERIFIED). The gift orientation suggests a practical consumables restock may be unclaimed space, but that is inference, and so is the idea that nurse boxes churn fast; the only complaint found is a generic one not about nurses. **Subscription fatigue is real**: a NerdWallet survey, reported via local TV, found **55% of Americans plan to significantly cut back subscriptions in 2026** ([KTTC](https://www.kttc.com/2026/04/07/more-than-half-americans-plan-cut-subscriptions-2026-survey-finds/), search summary), which makes easy skip, pause and cancel table stakes. Price sensitivity may be real for a sizeable share: the Nurse.org 2026 survey (2,090 nurses and students, not nationally representative) found roughly **a quarter say pay barely covers or does not cover essential monthly expenses**, and 37% worked extra shifts for financial reasons ([Nurse.org](https://nurse.org/articles/state-of-nursing-survey-2026); [Advisory Board](https://www.advisory.com/daily-briefing/2026/05/06/nurse-survey), search summaries). That points toward low starter prices and gift purchases (Nurses Week in May and December) as top-of-funnel, not yet validated.

What the research did not establish matters more. There are **no quotes in the target women's own words**: Reddit, allnurses, Etsy and Trustpilot pages were not reachable, so there is no nurse-specific subscription attitude, willingness-to-pay figure, churn, refund rate or repeat rate. Nothing was found about women over 40 working nights beyond a median RN age of 50 ([NCSBN](https://ncsbn.org/research/recent-research/workforce/2024-workforce-rn.page)) and a Sermo finding, from a UK page, that over a third of respondents had not found a scrub brand they liked ([Sermo](https://www.sermo.com/en-gb/resources/the-best-scrubs-for-women-in-clinical-practice/)). The discretion, not-juvenile and one-tidy-restock angles are hypotheses to test, not findings.

## A low-cost validation plan with thresholds from the model

Every step below is a draft for the owner to approve; no agent sends, posts, pays or publishes, and nothing here recommends inventory, branding or advertising spend before the compliance gate is recorded as cleared in `work/decisions.md`. The owner must decide whether the gate, written for the pouch business, also covers a non-pouch line.

**Step 1, survey and mining (cost: time).** Draft a short survey of overnight women nurses asking what they re-buy, how often, what they last paid, and whether they have cancelled a subscription and why; include a recurring-versus-one-time price question for the planner. Separately and manually, mine Reddit (r/nursing) and Facebook-group threads for stated needs, because no agent reached them. Recommended thresholds: a stated replacement interval of 3 to 6 months for socks from a clear majority of respondents (the model's quarterly cadence depends on it), and a stated willingness to pay a recurring price near the model's assumptions. The notes give no sample size or pass rate; the owner should set them before looking at results.

**Step 2, landing page with waitlist or pre-sale (cost: payment fees and a page).** Test the digital membership at $6.99 and the sock offer at $34.99 on a landing page with a waitlist or pre-sale, using comfort-only wording reviewed by the label-claims reviewer. The kill rule for digital is simple: if buyers convert only at one-time prices of $3 to $10, drop the recurring plan. For acquisition, hold the model's CAC lines: $25 hard ceiling, $40 fail line, and the concept's own break-even CAC ($38.73 for socks, $50.17 for digital).

**Step 3, samples (cost: sample orders).** Order samples from two or three suppliers (a small-MOQ sock importer, a Printful printed sock as the fallback, and one tea or snack wholesaler). For socks, the kill or continue test is landed cost per pair at or below $7.51 (zero contribution), with $5.50 as the level that earns $6.03 per order, plus delivery time and a size-return rate from a 20 to 30 unit pilot. For the snack box, the test is a wholesale quote at about 50% of retail or better and a 3PL kitting quote, with 100% of retail failing at $2.22 per order. Re-sample the exact variant on a schedule.

**Step 4, small pilot with a stated budget (owner sets the number).** The notes give no pilot budget and I will not invent one. A pilot of 20 to 30 sock subscribers with a Shopify Subscriptions app at $0, a single supplier and one checked cancellation flow is the unit the notes describe. Do not move to Recharge ($99 monthly) until the base-case subscriber counts that cover its fee (22 for socks, 16 for digital, 15 for snacks) are plausible.

**The Delphine caffeine pouch plan.** The comparator earns $29.61 over 12 months at base inputs (range $58.94 to $6.72), similar to the best physical concepts, but it carries an unresolved compliance gate, a large inventory requirement, state-law, ad-platform and payment-processor questions the earlier research left open, and the highest regulatory rating in the compliance note. This analysis cannot justify funding it. The practical choice is to park the pouch plan as a later line and, if a non-regulated launch product builds the nurse audience and email list first, revisit it after the gate clears (a judgment, not a finding). Whether to pause pouch work while the tests run is the owner's decision. The Delphine name is itself uncleared by a trademark attorney, and any new brand name for the nurse line is uncleared.

**For a professional.** Lawyer: whether graduated or mmHg-labeled socks sold with comfort-only copy are treated as devices under 21 CFR 880.5780 and how the 2026 General Wellness policy applies; MoCRA responsible-person duties and the $1M small-business test for any cosmetic; whether any sleep-, shift- or fatigue-adjacent framing avoids disease-claim and house-rule problems, or supplements and OTC items should be excluded; who is the manufacturer, packer or distributor when the brand repacks third-party items in a box; whether nurse creators can say "as a nurse" without the brand owning health claims, and which state board rules apply to "RN" and "nurse-approved"; how to build a profession-targeted audience on Meta, TikTok and Google without implying a health condition; and a 50-state auto-renewal review with a compliant cancellation flow and processor terms. Trademark attorney: clearance of the final name and any "nurse", "night" or "rest" terms and third-party brand mentions. Accountant: sales tax by category and state for physical subscriptions, dropship nexus and resale certificates (roughly 10 to 15 states are reported to require a certificate tied to their own registration, from [Mondaq](https://admin.mondaq.com/unitedstates/sales-taxes-vat-gst/807858/how-the-wayfair-ruling-weighs-down-drop-shippers-what-you-need-to-know), secondary), and cost of goods for low-ticket consumables. Insurance broker: product-liability and recall coverage by category, vendor certificates and additional-insured terms, and coverage for imported goods and food.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "which product" to "does this audience pay for a subscription at all." The model says the answer is unlikely to come from small consumables: $5.50 to $8.40 of postage plus $2.50 pick/pack makes any physical item priced under about $30 per shipment a loser, so the winning shapes are a higher-ticket quarterly parcel (socks) or no parcel at all (digital). The digital planner is the cleanest read on demand because it removes cost and inventory from the equation, yet its $6.99 price is the least supported number in the report. Socks are the strongest physical candidate precisely because the nurse-specific evidence is strongest there, and also the category where one careless word turns a comfort product into a regulated device.

The honest position is that no concept has earned money yet, in the model or in the market. Costs are benchmarks, prices are assumptions, and the audience has not been heard in its own words. A few weeks of survey work, manual thread mining, a waitlist page and two or three sample orders will move more of these numbers from assumption to evidence than any further desk research, and they cost little. Until then the compliance gate, the uncleared name and the lawyer, accountant and broker questions above stay open, and the pouch line stays parked.
