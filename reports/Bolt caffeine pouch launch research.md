# Bolt's name and category status block launch

**Date checked for all items: 2026-10-27.** This report presents findings and open questions. It does not conclude that anything is legal, compliant, safe or clear. The age policy is not yet recorded in `work/decisions.md`, so this report treats Bolt as 21+, per the project rules.

The most important finding is that the name "Bolt" is the largest launch risk, ahead of federal regulation. Bolt Nutrition LLC holds two live US registrations for BOLT covering dietary and nutritional supplements (Class 5) and online retail (Class 35), both registered 2026-05-19 ([Trademarkia 99319906](https://www.trademarkia.com/bolt-99319906)). A caffeine pouch is plausibly a Class 5 supplement, and an operator at boltpouches.com already sells "BOLT Energy Pouches" in the same product format. The second finding is that no US regulator has said what a caffeine pouch is. No FDA statement, warning letter or rule aimed at caffeine pouches surfaced, and whether a pouch held in the mouth and not swallowed is a "dietary supplement" or a conventional food is unresolved. That question drives labeling, manufacturing rules, adverse-event duties, tax and processor treatment ([21 USC 321, Cornell mirror](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/321)). Third, no state law written specifically for caffeine pouches was found, but state coverage is partial: 30 states were never searched individually. Fourth, 25 mg is not unique, because Grinds already sells a 25 mg option at about $0.33 per pouch. Fifth, ad, payment, SMS and carrier policies are mostly unverified, and the closest look-alike language (Meta's ban on products that "simulate smoking") is a real risk for pouch-format creative. The compliance gate in the project plan is not cleared by this research. Do not commit money to inventory, branding or advertising until a lawyer, a trademark attorney, an accountant and an insurance broker have answered the questions at the end.

## Nobody at FDA has said what a caffeine pouch is, and that classification drives everything downstream

The notes found no FDA warning letter, rule or statement about caffeine pouches. The only on-point caffeine enforcement verified was aimed at pure and liquid caffeine, for example the 2021 Trippo International letter ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/trippo-international-llc-612586-03102021)). This was seen via search snippets, and FDA's warning-letter database was not queried directly, so absence is not clearance. Trade and vendor sources say pouches are sold as supplements outside tobacco authority, but these are secondary and UNVERIFIED ([Tobacco Insider](https://tobaccoinsider.com/caffeine-pouches-questions-and-answers/)). The statutory definition of a dietary supplement requires a product "intended for ingestion in a form described in" 21 USC 350(c)(1)(B)(i) and one that is not "represented for use as a conventional food" ([Cornell LII, 21 USC 321](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/321), a mirror of the Code). Whether a pouch that is held in the mouth satisfies the ingestion element is contested by secondary sources and not resolved by any FDA text found. A certification-industry blog argues pouches may fall outside the definition, but the page that was fetched did not contain that analysis, so the argument is UNVERIFIED ([BSCG](https://www.bscg.org/blogs/single/energy-pouches-safety-ingredients-and-compliance-risk)). The text of 350(c)(1)(B)(i) was not retrieved.

The stakes are concrete. If Bolt is a supplement, 21 CFR 101.36 requires a Supplement Facts panel on which caffeine would appear with "Daily Value not established" ([eCFR 101.36](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-10-20/title-21.xml?part=101&section=101.36)), and cGMP under 21 CFR Part 111 applies to anyone who manufactures, packages, labels or holds the product, including product made by one firm and labeled by another. Part 111 also requires verifying that each finished batch meets strength specifications, which for Bolt means mg caffeine per pouch ([eCFR 111.1](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-10-20/title-21.xml?part=111&section=111.1); [eCFR 111.75](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-10-20/title-21.xml?part=111&section=111.75)). The brand on the label becomes the "responsible person" who must report serious adverse events within 15 business days, keep records for six years and print a domestic address or phone number ([21 USC 379aa-1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/379aa-1), Cornell mirror). A "dietary supplement" statement of identity and allergen "Contains" labeling also apply under 21 USC 343 (exact subsection text and the FASTER Act sesame rule were not retrieved). Any structure/function wording needs the 21 CFR 101.93 disclaimer and a 30-day FDA notice, which is moot if Bolt makes no such claim, but counsel must decide what counts as one for "energy" language ([eCFR 101.93](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-10-20/title-21.xml?part=101&section=101.93)).

If a pouch is instead a conventional food, the only caffeine GRAS listing found is 21 CFR 182.1180, limited to cola-type beverages at 0.02 percent ([eCFR 182.1180](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-10-20/title-21.xml?part=182&section=182.1180)). No GRAS notice for caffeine in pouches was found. That is the reading of the notes' author, not an FDA statement. The NDI question (whether caffeine counts as a pre-1994 ingredient in this delivery form) is UNVERIFIED because FDA's NDI page returned 404.

FDA's other signals are guidance, not rules. The 2018 "Highly Concentrated Caffeine in Dietary Supplements" guidance is final, non-binding, and aimed at bulk pure caffeine, resting on the adulteration theory of "significant or unreasonable risk" ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/guidance-industry-highly-concentrated-caffeine-dietary-supplements)). FDA's consumer page cites **400 mg per day** as an amount not generally associated with negative effects in healthy adults ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much)). A draft "Labeling Caffeine Content in Foods and Beverages" guidance was added to FDA's 2026 agenda on June 29, 2026, with no text or date, and whether it would reach supplements or pouches is unknown ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/hfp-constituent-updates/fda-releases-2026-human-foods-program-guidance-agenda); [Daily Coffee News](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2026/07/17/fda-eyes-caffeine-labeling-as-a-2026-priority/)). Bolt prints caffeine per pouch regardless. Press attention is rising: an AP report on October 22, 2026 says there is no federal minimum purchase age and that schools are banning pouches ([CP24/AP](https://www.cp24.com/news/world/2026/10/22/some-us-schools-are-banning-caffeine-pouches-heres-what-parents-and-students-should-know/)). The only federal caffeine bill found, H.R. 2511 (Sarah Katz Caffeine Safety Act), shows as referred to House Energy and Commerce on 2025-03-31 with no confirmed movement; congress.gov returned 403, so its status is unread directly ([Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/2511); [GovTrack](https://www.govtrack.us/congress/bills/119/hr2511)).

Other federal items are smaller. The CPSC child-resistant packaging list at 16 CFR 1700.14 does not mention caffeine in the text retrieved, so child-resistant tins would be voluntary unless a state or agency acts, which is an owner and counsel decision ([eCFR 1700.14](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-10-20/title-16.xml?part=1700&section=1700.14)). For advertising, FTC health-claim substantiation, the 2023 Endorsement Guides (16 CFR 255, material connections must be disclosed) and the Reviews Rule (16 CFR 465, in force since 2024-10-21) apply ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance); [eCFR 16 CFR 255](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-10-20/title-16.xml?part=255); [FTC Reviews Rule Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)). A subscribe-and-save offer falls under ROSCA's disclosure, consent and simple-cancellation requirements ([Cornell LII, 15 USC 8403](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/8403)). The FTC negative-option rule was reopened in 2026 (docket FTC-2026-0265), and a law-firm source says the 2024 click-to-cancel amendments were vacated in July 2025, which is UNVERIFIED against the opinion ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/negative-option-rule); [BCLP](https://www.bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/im-not-dead-yet-is-the-demise-of-the-ftcs-click-to-cancel-exaggerated.html)). A single-source item on FDA leadership changes is UNVERIFIED and not relied on.

## No state pouch law found, but 30 states were never individually searched

No enacted or pending state law naming caffeine pouches was found across both state passes ([ABC11/6abc explainer](https://6abc.com/post/what-are-caffeine-pouches-schools-banning/19169168/)). The nearest items turn on definitions and on how Bolt describes itself. Delaware HB 394 would have barred under-18 sales of "caffeinated dietary supplements", but its House substitute, passed unanimously around June 11-12, 2026, replaced the ban with Division of Public Health signage that retailers are "encouraged" to display. No Senate action was found, the term "caffeinated dietary supplements" is undefined in the sources read, and one source describes the original ban without confirming its removal, so the final shape is UNVERIFIED ([DE House Dems](https://housedems.delaware.gov/2026/06/12/house-passes-neal-bill-to-better-regulate-highly-caffeinated-beverages/); [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/delawares-house-representatives-passes-caffeine-warning-signage-bill)). Companion HB 396 (menu warning symbols) has no status found ([DE House GOP](https://housegop.delaware.gov/2026/05/02/caffeine-regulation-bills-delaware/)).

The weight-loss and muscle-building supplement age laws are the second cluster. New York's law (GBL 391-oo) bans sales to under-18s of supplements "labeled, marketed, or otherwise represented" for weight loss or muscle building and covers online delivery sales. A cert petition by the Council for Responsible Nutrition was reported filed in March 2026 ([public.law](https://newyork.public.law/laws/n.y._general_business_law_section_391-oo); [EBG](https://www.ebglaw.com/health-law-advisor/states-further-legislation-to-restrict-sale-of-weight-loss-muscle-building-products-to-minors)). California AB 2030 was reported signed 2026-09-28, with online sellers required to verify age through government-source data or adult-signature shipping and civil penalties up to $500 per violation. Its effective date is not stated in the sources and no chaptered text was read ([Haynes Boone](https://www.haynesboone.com/news/alerts/new-california-law-bans-sale-of-diet-pills-and-certain-supplements-to-minors)). This corrects the first pass, which carried the California item as a doubtful snippet. Pending bills include New Jersey A 5487, Rhode Island S 2774 and Michigan HB 5250 ([Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/11/new-michigan-bill-targets-youth-access-to-weight-loss-and-muscle-building-supplements); [RI bill text](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/BillText26/SenateText26/S2774.htm)). None mentions caffeine in the sources read, which is why Bolt's copy discipline matters: these laws key on marketing claims, and the project already bars performance and weight-loss claims. Older narrow laws target powdered pure caffeine, such as Illinois 410 ILCS 647 (under-18, with a pouch likely outside the definition by the notes' inference) and a Suffolk County, NY local law ([ILGA](https://ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/documents/041006470K10.htm); [CBS NY](https://www.cbsnews.com/newyork/news/suffolk-county-votes-to-ban-sales-of-powered-caffeine-to-minors/)). Beverage-focused bills (MA H.1908, CT HB 5269, NJ A3259, PA HB 2377, Indiana SB 369) have unconfirmed status and mostly address drinks ([MA bill](https://malegislature.gov/Bills/194/H1908.Html)).

Nicotine-pouch laws do not on their face reach caffeine-only products in the summaries read, but only summaries were read. Oregon SB 1571 (effective 2026-06-05) expands "tobacco products" to oral nicotine pouches, and whether nicotine is a required element was not confirmed from statute text ([Oregon OHA](https://www.oregon.gov/oha/erd/pages/oregon-law-expands-tobacco-products-definition-to-prevent-underage-sales-youth-nicotine-addiction-06.03.2026.aspx)). The risk the notes identify is practical: look-alike packaging and schools treating pouches as simulated tobacco. For example, Holton schools (KS) treat them under tobacco rules, and a Michigan middle school said they "closely resemble nicotine pouches" ([ABC7 Chicago](https://abc7chicago.com/post/what-are-caffeine-pouches-schools-banning/19169168/); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/10/22/schools-ban-caffeine-pouches-students-coffee/)). No state AG action on pouches was found; the Texas AG energy-drink probe concerns drinks ([TX OAG](https://oag.state.tx.us/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-announces-investigation-celsius-energy-drink-company-protect-texas)).

**Coverage is partial and the gaps are material.** Thirty states were never searched individually: AL, AK, AZ, AR, CO, HI, ID, IA, KY, LA, ME, MD, MN, MS, MT, NE, NV, NH, NM, ND, OK, OR (incidental hit only), SC, SD, TN, UT, VT, VA, WV and WY. Read these as "not searched", not "no law". Florida, Ohio, Georgia and North Carolina were priority states but received no specific searches. LegiScan and NCSL full-text searches were not accessible, so a bill filed under "stimulant" or "energy product" wording could be missed. State sales tax, supplement registration and Prop 65 are essentially unresearched: caffeine appears as "under consideration" on OEHHA's page per a search summary, the page itself did not load, and ingredient-level screening needs the formula ([OEHHA](https://oehha.ca.gov/proposition-65/chemicals/caffeine)). Sales-tax treatment by state comes only from 2021-era vendor blogs and must be confirmed with revenue departments ([TaxJar](https://blog.taxjar.com/sales-tax-by-states-are-dietary-supplements-taxable/)). No state wholesale licensing or online age-verification standard was verified.

## Ad platforms, payments and carriers are mostly unverified, with Meta's look-alike language the clearest risk

Only a handful of official policy texts were read, so this section is largely a list of questions to put to each provider in writing. Meta's tobacco policy prohibits ads promoting nicotine products, lists nicotine pouches, and also bars "products that simulate smoking, such as vapes, including products that don't contain tobacco or nicotine". The page does not use the word "lookalike" or address non-nicotine pouches ([Meta](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/tobacco-related-products/)). Meta's supplement rules were not found on the drugs page that was fetched. An agency summary says nicotine-free caffeine pouches are not auto-blocked and fall under general unsafe-substances review, which is a search snippet only ([adsuploader](https://adsuploader.com/blog/meta-ad-guidelines)). A brand-published claim from Roon, a competitor with an interest, says nicotine-free products are eligible for standard paid ads on Meta, YouTube and Twitch, conflicting with a third-party claim that Meta bars even nicotine-free alternatives ([Roon](https://www.takeroon.com/blog/nicotine-pouch-streamer-sponsorship-ad-restrictions); [AdAmigo](https://www.adamigo.ai/blog/meta-ads-policy-tobacco-ad-guidelines-2025)). Google's tobacco policy covers products "designed to simulate tobacco smoking", and its page was truncated before any mention of pouches ([Google Ads](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16489929?hl=en)). Google's "Unapproved substances" section exists, with violations carrying suspension "without prior warning" per a search summary, and caffeine is not named in what was retrieved ([Google](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031)). TikTok Ads, TikTok Shop, YouTube, X, Reddit, Snapchat and Pinterest could not be verified (TikTok official pages errored or rendered blank). The Cyclone Pods podcast listing, about a zero-nicotine vape business with a planned caffeine "Focus Pouch", claims every ad platform banned its founder and says paid ads were "off the table". That concerns the vape business, not caffeine pouch ads ([Unofficial Shopify Podcast](https://ethercycle.com/blogs/unofficial-shopify-podcast/the-restricted-goods-playbook-no-ads-no-stripe-no-usps)). No credible public report of a caffeine pouch brand being rejected or banned by a platform, marketplace or processor was found, and which processors and ad channels competitors actually use is unknown.

On payments, Shopify Payments defers to each processor's prohibited-business list and calls its own lists representative, not exhaustive ([Shopify Payments terms](https://www.shopify.com/legal/terms-payments-us)). Stripe's list, as returned, includes tobacco products and "herbal and vitamin e-cigarettes", says US merchants may only sell products FDA-authorized in some categories, and prohibits "pseudo-pharmaceuticals or nutraceuticals that are not safe or make harmful claims", without naming caffeine ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses)). Tower Payments launched a high-risk service for online nicotine-pouch sellers in January 2026, noting many processors block nicotine sellers ([Digital Transactions](https://www.digitaltransactions.net/tower-payments-launches-new-solution-for-online-nicotine-pouch-sellers)). PayPal and Braintree wording is partial (search summary), Square's US page returned 429, and Authorize.net and card-network high-risk lists were not reached. No fee, reserve or underwriting figures were found, so none are given. For email and SMS, Klaviyo's acceptable use policy prohibits "illegal substances" and tobacco-type categories on short codes without addressing supplements or nicotine pouches, so 10DLC registration for a pouch product is a question for Klaviyo and the carrier ([Klaviyo AUP](https://www.klaviyo.com/legal/acceptable-use-policy)). Mailchimp was not read. For marketplaces, Shopify's AUP text returned had no prohibited-product list, Amazon lists caffeine pouches (a price tracker shows Nectr Energy) while its supplement approval rules are third-party-sourced, and Walmart carries Wip ([CamelCamelCamel](https://camelcamelcamel.com/product/B0F7RWLFZD); [FITT Insider](https://insider.fitt.co/press-release/wip-energy-pouches-now-available-in-over-330-walmart-fuel-centers-nationwide/)).

For carriers, USPS Publication 52 defines cigarette, smokeless tobacco ("intended to be placed in the oral or nasal cavity") and ENDS, with a 21-year minimum age to send or receive covered products. Nicotine pouches are not named, and a tobacco-free, nicotine-free pouch does not obviously fall inside these definitions ([USPS Pub 52](https://pe.usps.com/text/pub52/pub52c4_026.htm)). UPS returned "Access Denied" (the first pass cited a UPS list from a search snippet, so UPS is UNVERIFIED), and FedEx and DHL were not read. Whether any carrier treats a pouch as tobacco-like at acceptance, and whether adult-signature options are needed, is open. The recommended approach is to ask each processor, ad platform and carrier for written confirmation covering "caffeine oral pouch, 25 mg, 21+ gated" with product photos in hand.

A low-dose product used several times daily therefore costs more per mg than the high-dose brands, which matters for pricing a multi-use product. Ultra raised an $11M Series A in January 2026 and says it sold 1M cans in six months (company claim), and Wip is in 330+ Walmart Fuel Centers, which shows convenience-channel proof ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260120688296/en/Pouches-Minus-the-Nicotine-Ultra-Raises-11-Million-to-Build-a-Clean-Non-Addictive-Pouch-for-High-Performers/)).

Published market sizes disagree by more than a factor of ten: about $66.5M for 2025 (360iResearch), about $500M (Market Report Analytics) and $1,061.1M (DRI, UNVERIFIED snippet, page 404) ([360iResearch](https://www.360iresearch.com/library/intelligence/caffeine-pouches); [MRA](https://www.marketreportanalytics.com/reports/caffeine-pouches-194756); [DRI](https://www.dri.co.jp/auto/report/oga/251111-caffeine-energy-pouch-market-outlook.html)). Treat the order of magnitude as unknown. Margins, wholesale terms and velocity have no published data, and adjacent formats (gum, mints, strips, chews) were not surveyed beyond PROBAR Bolt at 20 mg per serving. Customer evidence is thin and second-hand: Roon's summary of 13 Reddit threads reports mouth irritation (NZE, Nectr), flavor fade (Grinds), skepticism about herbal pouches and a hard-to-cancel Ultra subscription. No thread asked for a 25 to 50 mg product, so demand for 25 mg is a hypothesis to test by survey or waitlist, not a finding ([Roon Reddit summary](https://www.takeroon.com/blog/nootropic-pouches-reddit-what-users-say)).

**Dose math.** Using FDA's 400 mg benchmark and the notes' arithmetic, 25 mg pouches add up as follows. Figures count pouch caffeine only; other caffeine intake must be added.

| Pouches per day | mg per day | Share of 400 mg | mg left under 400 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 100 | 25% | 300 |
| 8 | 200 | 50% | 200 |
| 12 | 300 | 75% | 100 |
| 16 | 400 | 100% | 0 |

One 25 mg pouch is half of a 50 mg pouch, a quarter of Wip 100 mg and about 1/7 of Ultra's 180 mg. A 12-oz brewed coffee is 113 to 247 mg and a caffeinated soft drink 23 to 83 mg, per FDA, so 12 pouches equal roughly 1.2 to 2.7 coffees (the notes' arithmetic) ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much)). Daily-total wording on the label is for counsel to approve. Pharmacokinetics are uncertain: gum and spray studies exist, but no peer-reviewed study of caffeine pouches was found, so delivered dose per pouch is unknown and competitor "100% bioavailability" and onset claims are unsourced marketing ([PubMed 11839447](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11839447/)). Mayo Clinic and USDA figures were not retrieved.

## Manufacturers are few, unverified, and one has an FDA warning letter

Only five contract-manufacturer candidates publicly advertise caffeine pouches: JD Quality Pouches (Virginia, US), Daily Manufacturing Solutions (California, US), Global Snus (Lithuania), Luna Corporate (Poland) and House of Pouches (Denmark). The target of 8 to 10 was not met. The gap pass found only two weak non-pouch leads: HTT Packaging (sachets and stick packs) and Gumtech (gum), both unconfirmed. No certification for any candidate is verified: FDA Food Facility Registration, NSF directory, Virginia SCC and VDACS, FDA import alerts and the recall database were not queried.

**Daily Manufacturing Solutions** is the new gap-pass finding. FDA's Center for Tobacco Products issued warning letter ER2601477 on 2026-04-21 for manufacturing and offering PUCK COOL MINT 6MG nicotine pouches without a marketing authorization order, classing them as adulterated and misbranded and giving 15 working days to respond ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/daily-manufacturing-solutions-inc-728255-04212026)). The letter concerns nicotine, not caffeine, and whether the firm responded or the letter was closed is not found. Its own site claims "FDA Registered Facility" and "GMP Certified", caffeine pouches at 50 to 150 mg (25 mg is not among the strengths listed, so fill feasibility is unconfirmed), a **minimum order of 25,000 cans with 5,000 cans per flavor** and lead time "as low as 6 weeks", all self-reported ([dlymfg.com](https://dlymfg.com)). That is the only published MOQ for any candidate. Addresses conflict across sources (La Verne, Rancho Cucamonga, Fontana, and one directory also lists Rockwell NC). A Thomasnet profile lists FDA registration with a certificate date but no registration number, so that claim is UNVERIFIED ([Thomasnet](https://www.thomasnet.com/company/daily-manufacturing-solutions-inc-30869349/profile)). Whether a maker that also runs nicotine lines is acceptable, and whether line segregation can be assured, is an owner and counsel decision. This corrects the first pass, which located the firm only through a ZoomInfo snippet.

**JD Quality Pouches** claims NSF, USDA Organic, a Virginia food manufacturing license and HACCP on its own site and offers formulation through canning, but does not mention cGMP or FDA registration, and states no MOQ. Its 646 phone code does not match Virginia and its page says both Oilville and Richmond, which the notes flag for verification, not as proof of a problem ([jdqualitypouches.com](https://jdqualitypouches.com)). **Global Snus** says only "GMP certified", which is not the same as Part 111 compliance for a supplement. Global Snus, Luna and House of Pouches publish no MOQ, price, lead time, sample policy or testing ([globalsnus.com](https://globalsnus.com); [Wonnda House of Pouches](https://wonnda.com/de/lieferanten/house-of-pouches/)). No FDA letter naming Global Snus or Luna surfaced, but the database was not queried. Competitor Roon states a US NSF-certified, FDA-registered, cGMP plant without naming it, and NSF "certifies the facility, not the product" ([Roon](https://www.takeroon.com/pouches/where-its-made-and-who-tested-it)).

Costs are essentially unknown. No credible US co-packer price per tin or lead time is published. A China sourcing agent's blog gives self-contradicting ranges with undefined units, so no number from it should be used ([Sourcify](https://www.sourcifychina.com/caffeine-pouch-manufacturer/)). Caffeine anhydrous reference prices are lab-scale or unverified marketplace listings, with no verified bulk food-grade supplier, fleece supplier, sweetener supplier or tin maker named. Child-resistant tin options (for example the Chemular add-on announced August 2026) are an emerging area ([Packaging Gateway](https://www.packaging-gateway.com/news/chemular-child-resistant-pouches-packaging-nicotine/)). Dosing 25 mg in a small pouch makes fill homogeneity a question for content-uniformity data. That is the notes' assumption, not sourced. The draft RFQ and five supplier-call questions in the sourcing notes remain unsent drafts for the owner's review; their core asks are the maker's regulatory framework (Part 111 or food cGMP), assay method and tolerance for 25 mg, price breaks with setup fees separated, materials and COAs, and tin, child-resistant, sample and recall-insurance terms.

## Positioning and search: the open lane is label-forward and adult, and "Bolt" alone is a weak search term

Competitor language is overwhelmingly "bold", "clean energy", "no crash" and price-versus-energy-drink, which Bolt cannot use because the project bars focus, performance and "no crash" claims. Wip's homepage uses "bold" and candy-like flavors with no age statement ([Wip](https://www.wip.com)). Relative to what was seen, the less crowded territory is a published per-pouch mg figure with no proprietary blend, since Roon itself calls undisclosed doses a red flag ([Roon ranked](https://www.takeroon.com/blog/best-caffeine-pouches-ranked)), and a calm, adult, routine-oriented voice. Grinds at 25 mg and FlowBlend at 25 mg with L-theanine (surfaced in an AI-answer tracker) show the low-dose slot is thin, not empty ([Parse](https://www.parse.gl/prompts/p/im-looking-for-a-convenient-pocket-sized-source-of-caffeine-and-focus--8f28c005-4e13-409e-8ce6-7d6337023d2a)). The youth-appeal environment is hostile: TikTok tags such as "lip pillow" and "upper decky" are used, and schools say pouches resemble nicotine pouches ([ABC7 Chicago](https://abc7chicago.com/post/what-are-caffeine-pouches-schools-banning/19169168/)). Brands such as Ultra set 18+, so a 21+ standard is more conservative than competitors; nicotine-lookalike tin design, candy cues, slang, gaming or school-adjacent channels, and any quit-smoking or nicotine-alternative framing should be avoided. FDA has authorized nicotine pouches only as listed (43 products as of the notes' check), and "nicotine alternative" language would add regulatory risk ([FDA](https://fda.gov/tobacco-products/market-and-distribute-tobacco-product/nicotine-pouch-products-authorized-fda)).

For search, Amazon US demand for "caffeine pouches" is about 30,600 searches per month (MerchantWords, undated; Amazon, not Google) ([MerchantWords](https://www.merchantwords.com/search/us/caffeine)). Google Trends, Ahrefs and Similarweb data were unavailable (authentication or blocks), so no Google volume or backlink data exists in the notes. Results for generic queries are dominated by brand-run listicles, with Roon's ranked page (comparison table, honest-limitation notes, FAQ, affiliate link) the model content type, plus thin auto-generated pages on edu subdomains that should not be treated as quality benchmarks ([Takeroon](https://www.takeroon.com/blog/best-caffeine-pouches-ranked)). The notes' inference is that a transparent per-pouch milligram comparison with a published method, a "how to read the label" explainer and factual caffeine comparisons, all free of performance claims, is the content gap. The exact phrase "bolt pouch" is contested by tool, firearm and gaming meanings (for example the RuneScape wiki), so "Bolt" alone will be hard to rank and a distinctive modifier would help search as well as trademark separation ([OSRS wiki](https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/w/Bolt_pouch)).

## Conclusion

The notes change the picture in one specific way: the risks that can stop the launch are not the ones the first-pass leads emphasized. Federal regulation is quiet, with no pouch-specific FDA action, and the state picture is sparse. But "Bolt" is already registered in supplements by another company and already sold as a caffeine pouch brand by an unidentified operator, so the name is the first thing to resolve, and it should be settled before a single design or ad dollar is spent. The classification question is the second gate because a single legal answer determines which rules, which tax treatment and which processor category apply.

The notes also show how much remains unknown. Official policy text was read for only three of about twenty-two requested channels, state coverage skipped thirty states, no supplier certification was verified and market-size estimates differ by more than tenfold. The most reliable moves are the cheap, definitive ones: written confirmations from processors and ad platforms, direct database checks that tools could not perform, a trademark opinion and a demand test for 25 mg. The 25 mg dose is a plausible but unproven differentiator, since Grinds already sells it, customers have not been shown to ask for it, and its per-mg cost is higher than every high-dose rival's.
