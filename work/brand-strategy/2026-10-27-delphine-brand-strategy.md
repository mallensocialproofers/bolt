## 1. Positioning statement and audience profile

### Positioning (two sentences)
Delphine is a 25 mg caffeine pouch for adult women who prefer their small daily things plain, well made and clearly labeled. Where other pouches lead with effect promises or nicotine-style strength cues, Delphine leads with a printed number, a published price per pouch and a tin that looks like it belongs on a vanity or in a handbag.

(Both sentences are claim-free by design. They name no effect, no occupation and no time of day. Still subject to claims review.)

### The "healthy halo" request: answer
The owner asked earlier for a healthy halo. The answer is no health words, and CLAUDE.md rule 4 stands unless the owner changes it in writing. What is available instead is a botanical or elegant feel carried by design alone: muted earthy color, plant-inspired pattern or line work, paper and metal textures, daylight photography. Do not use the words "natural", "pure", "clean", "wellness", "botanical benefits" or similar in copy. Even "natural" is a substantiation burden and an implied-claim risk (inference; counsel to confirm). Imagery can also imply a claim (FTC says names and imagery can imply claims; source: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance, as cited in the nurse report), so the botanical cue should be abstract and decorative, not herbs, leaves-as-ingredients or anything suggesting the pouch contains a plant extract unless it does and counsel approves.

### Audience profile (adults only)
Primary customer, as chosen by the owner: women who work overnight shifts, mainly nurses. Important: the brand must NOT say so. No "night", "shift", "nurse", scrubs or hospital imagery in copy or visuals (nurse report: implied-claim risk, and ANA says fatigue is an employer systems issue). So the audience is reached by where the brand shows up and by what it looks like, not by what it says.

What the sources support (all from the nurse report, checked 2026-10-27 per that report):
- About 3.47 million RNs in the US; median pay $97,550; roughly 87-89% women (BLS https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/registered-nurses.htm; USAFacts https://usafacts.org/answers/how-many-nurses-are-there-in-the-us/).
- NCSBN 2024 (744,714 RN respondents): median age 50, median pretax earnings $88,000, 53% in hospitals (https://ncsbn.org/research/recent-research/workforce/2024-workforce-rn.page). The page does not report female share or night share.
- Caffeine use is near-universal in the nurse samples found (88.1% of 182 US nurses, Phillips 2021: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9023427/table/Tab1). This supports category fit only.
- UNVERIFIED or unknown: share of RNs who work nights; how nurses buy caffeine; price tolerance; any verbatim nurse language; hospital policy on oral pouches. The three-shifts-a-week cadence is an assumption.

What this means for the brand (inference):
- The buyer is a working adult woman, with a median age near 50 in the nurse workforce. That argues against anything youthful, trendy or "girly". Elegant, composed and grown-up fits the data better than pastel or playful.
- A secondary audience, also inference: adult women who already treat coffee or tea as a small daily ritual and would like a tidy, discreet, well-designed alternative format. No data on this group was gathered.
- Purchase drivers the claim-safe boundary allows: price per pouch, format, flavor, the printed 25 mg, tin and pocket convenience, a flexible skip/pause subscription, and possibly a verified-healthcare-worker price (mechanic from the nurse report; wording and eligibility for counsel; the discount replaces, not stacks with, the subscription discount).
- Hospital gift shops and cafes are a possible wholesale channel (nurse report, UNVERIFIED markup data). Hospital policy on selling or using the product on campus is unknown.

Customer worries to answer plainly on the site (inference, not research): "Does this look like a nicotine pouch?" (it should not), "How much caffeine am I getting in a day?" (daily-total wording is counsel's), "Is this for me?" (21+ gate and adult tone).

### Differentiation from Lucy (girl's-name nicotine brand)
A girl's first name for an oral pouch is a category habit Lucy already uses (batch2). Risks: shoppers or retailers may lump Delphine with nicotine pouches. Differentiators to build in, all by design and plain labeling:
1. A longer, French, three-syllable name, always set in a refined serif, never a short bold word-stamp.
2. The words "caffeine pouches" on the front of every pack, directly under the wordmark.
3. Packaging unlike nicotine tins (section 5).
4. A statement that the product contains no nicotine: wording is for counsel. The nurse report warns that "nicotine-free" invites a comparison, while batch2 recommends it on pack. Both views are recorded; this is a counsel decision.
5. Never put Delphine near nicotine in any retail adjacency we control (wholesale terms should say so; owner/counsel decision).

## 3. Voice guide

### Tone
Calm, precise, warm, adult. Think a well-written label from a small maker: short sentences, concrete numbers, no hype, no exclamation marks, no emojis, no slang. Confident enough not to oversell. The brand speaks to "you" as a capable adult and never talks about her body, tiredness, work or time of day.

### Words to use
25 mg, per pouch, caffeine pouch, adults 21+, tin, pouch, flavor names (owner to supply), price, per pouch, bundle, subscription, skip, pause, change your date, labeled, printed, plain, simple, made for adults, slim, pocket-sized, ingredients, how many to a tin, total milligrams per tin, "about the dose".

### Words and framings to avoid
- Effect and body words: energy, energize, boost, kick, buzz, jolt, alert, awake, focus, sharp, calm, steady, wake, fresh, refresh, restore, recharge, stamina, power, fuel, performance, productivity, "get through", "no crash", "crash-free", clean, pure, natural, wellness, healthy, detox, vitality, glow, balance, lift, support.
- Time and occupation: night, overnight, after hours, late, shift, "on shift", 3 a.m., nurse, nursing, scrubs, hospital, hero, front line, "thank you for your service", any "tired" or "exhausted" address. No nurse-as-expert endorsement or credentialed testimonial.
- Sleep and weight: sleep, rest, bedtime, insomnia, weight, appetite, slim-down (note: "slim" describing the tin is fine; do not apply it to people).
- Nicotine and quitting: nicotine alternative, switch, replace, quit, cravings, "tobacco-free" as a selling line, "smoke-free", "dip", "snus", "hit", "buzz", "strong".
- Youth and candy: sweet treat, candy, sprinkles, rainbow, fun, "vibes", "slay", "bestie", "girl boss", "queen", "obsessed", "game changer", "hack", emojis, pastel-cute language, mascots, dolphins or any marine cuteness.
- Heavy-use invitations: "stack", "unlimited", "as many as you like", "one more", "step up", "all day", "dose up".
- Safety or permission: "safe", "approved", "allowed at work", "doctor-recommended".
- Wholesome-halo words: "ritual" and "treat yourself" are borderline (indulgence cue); use sparingly and send to review.

## 5. Visual direction for a designer

Principle: describe the feeling, never copy another brand. All of this is hypothesis until tested. The earlier Bolt idea of making the dose number the largest element is DROPPED: the nurse report lists a strength number as the biggest element, with a single-color strength band, as a nicotine-tin cue. For Delphine the wordmark leads and "25 mg caffeine per pouch" is the second-largest, very legible element.

### Feel
Quiet, tactile, domestic-luxe: a pharmacy-free, hospital-free, nightlife-free look. Think a good stationery shop or a small perfume-and-tea counter, in daylight. Elegant, not precious; adult, not austere.

### Wordmark and type
- Wordmark: "Delphine" in title case or lowercase, set in a refined, slightly high-contrast serif. Avoid all-caps bold, avoid techy or rounded fonts, avoid script that looks like a wedding invitation.
- Supporting type: a clean humanist sans for dose, count, caution and website text. Keep body sizes legible; the "25 mg caffeine per pouch" statement and the 21+ statement should be unmissable (final sizes and placement are counsel's).
- No dolphin, wave, marine or Greek-column imagery. "Delphine" sounds like "dolphin" to some US readers (batch2), and marine cuteness reads young.

### Color (proposed, not tested)
- Base: warm ivory or paper white (for example around #F2EBDD).
- Accents: muted clay or terracotta, deep aubergine or ink-plum, dusty olive or sage-gray, with an antique brass or champagne metallic for small details.
- Flavor coding by tone of one or two accent colors per flavor, kept muted.
- Avoid: flat matte black, mint green and ice blue (nicotine-pouch coding per the nurse report), neon, bright pastels or any candy palette, saturated red, and pharmacy white-and-blue.
- The botanical cue: a restrained, abstract line pattern or single stem, embossed or printed tone-on-tone. Do not use a literal delphinium, herbs or fruit as ingredient imagery. (Delphinium is the flower associated with the name, but its toxicity and the word's etymology are general knowledge I did not verify; the safest route is to keep the plant cue abstract.)

### Packaging and tin
- Shape (hypothesis): a flat, slim rectangular tin with softly rounded corners, hinged or slide lid, sized to sit in a handbag or on a counter. This differs from the round, puck-style can that nicotine pouches commonly use. Counsel to review the look-alike risk and whether child-resistant features are advisable (CPSC special packaging list does not include caffeine, per decisions.md, which is not a conclusion that none applies).
- Finish: painted or printed metal in warm ivory or clay with a soft-touch or matte feel; embossed wordmark; one small brass-tone accent. Avoid glossy foil rainbow effects.
- Front-of-pack hierarchy: (1) wordmark, (2) "caffeine pouches", (3) "25 mg caffeine per pouch", (4) flavor name, (5) count and total mg per tin, (6) adults 21+. Warning, caution and nicotine statements per counsel on the back or underside.
- Inner pouch: plain, with "25 mg" printed on it (the dose-transparency promise). Pouch colors should be neutral, not candy colored.
- Outer carton or shipper: paper-based, ivory, minimal; includes the 21+ statement; no hospital or work imagery.
- Do not use: lip or mouth imagery, lightning, flame, clocks, moons, stars or night skies, medical crosses, stethoscopes, scrubs.

### Photo direction
- Natural daylight, soft shadows, real surfaces: linen, a tray, a ceramic cup beside the tin, a handbag on a chair. Still life first, hands second.
- When people appear: clearly adult women, visibly over 30, varied ages and backgrounds, relaxed and composed, in everyday clothes. No scrubs, no hospital settings, no night or dark rooms, no beds, no yawning or tired expressions, no gym or athletic shots.
- Never show the pouch being placed in or held at the mouth, and no close-ups of lips. Counsel to review how use is shown at all.
- No candy-like flat-lay colors, no confetti, no cartoon, no memes.

### Wordmark/identity cautions from the name screen
The name is shared with a restaurant (Class 43), a clothing brand (25/35), real estate and tax services (section 6). Do not design a mark that resembles any of their logos (I have not seen them) and do not sell clothing or open food-service ventures under the name without counsel.
